Project 03 · 20%

Experience / Interpret

phenomena · storyboard · site interpretation
Site: Moss Point, MSReview: Oct 10

Download the full Project 03 brief (PDF)

phenomenon — a fact or situation that is observed to exist or happen, esp. one whose cause or explanation is in question.
event — a thing that happens, esp. one of importance.

Experience

Sites are constantly in flux through a broad range of changes in phenomena. Temporal changes can occur through physical or perceptual alterations in spatial conditions. You will explore the Moss Point Waterfront looking for phenomena that occur on micro and macro scales. Identify the phenomena and the specific site conditions that make the phenomena possible.

Once you have identified your phenomena you will devise a method to capture and explain this event. You should take into consideration the following in order to develop a unique approach:

  • What is the length of the event?
  • What are the specific components that make the phenomena possible?
  • How is the site changed throughout the event?
  • Are there specific signals that occur?
  • Is the phenomena tied to another event?
  • Is the phenomena/event site specific?

Interpret

Storyboard — Based on your findings you will create a storyboard that illustrates the temporal quality of your site phenomena. The storyboard should consider a range of time and subdivide that time into meaningful markers throughout the illustration of the phenomena. You can use a combination of photographs, sketches, and diagrams to explain each frame of your storyboard. Be inventive in your use of the storyboard, combining analytical and experiential data together to develop the narrative of your site phenomena.

Physical Diagram — The diagram is a 3 or 4 dimensional representation of your phenomena. The diagram should be a simplified and structured representation of ideas, relationships, perceptions, anatomy, constructions, and/or statistical data. Your model does not have to conform to any scale but should be proportionally scalar.

Site Interpretation — Based on your researched phenomena you will create a site interpretation using models and/or drawings. Your site interpretation should be a representation of the site that is unique to your phenomena. The interpretation must conform to scalar proportions but should question the static conventions of the site model, plan, section, and elevation in order to focus on the site dynamics and experiences.
