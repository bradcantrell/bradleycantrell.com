LAR 7416 · Digital Practices IV

The concept of the diorama is explored through a single 90″–150″ long animation developed throughout the term. The animation is from a selected viewpoint — the orthographic plan or section, the axonometric, or the perspectival. The viewpoint should not change significantly throughout the animation, creating a limited view of the scene. The focus of the animation is on the elements, atmospheres, and effects that bring the landscape view to life.

Annotation & Notation

In conjunction with the animated landscape, each project also explores how to incorporate forms of annotation and notation to highlight real and perceived performance data, diagrammatic relationships, and didactic overlays. It is important that the correct balance of ambiguous experience and discreet diagramming are found to produce a compelling and engaging animation.

Media & Tools

The media and tools used to create the animation are determined by each student. The animation can be created directly in After Effects or may explore a hybrid solution that incorporates digital (3D animation, Rhino, 3ds Max, Maya) or physical models (stop-motion/time lapse). Each animation is guided and grounded in a narrative concept and series of technique and aesthetic precedents.
