LAR 7416 · Digital Practices IV

Digital Practices IV uses the diorama as a conceptual framework for representing landscape phenomena. The diorama refers to a three-dimensional representation of a scene or landscape, typically viewed from a controlled vantage point. Using a static viewpoint, each student will represent an animated view highlighting the ephemeral and temporal qualities of the landscape.

The Diorama

Representing landscape phenomena is fundamentally temporal, grounded in the notion that environments are continually in flux. People gather and disperse, the market rises, housing prices fall, and the seasons march along with the death of Winter into the life of the Spring, each signaling the march of time as day to night.

The diorama provides a grounded space to explore landscape phenomena through the construction of the orthographic projection. The viewpoint does not change significantly throughout the animation, creating a limited view of the scene — the focus stays on the elements, atmospheres, and effects that bring the landscape view to life.

Narrative & Precedents

Each animation is guided and grounded in a narrative concept and series of technique and aesthetic precedents. These may come from a broad range of interests — film, historic techniques, popular culture, etc. The cases are reviewed throughout the semester, and the final iteration should serve as an accompaniment to the animation/film as a series of cards or a small booklet designed within the overall aesthetic/narrative. It is feasible to focus on concept, precedent, and/or technique depending on interest.

Review 01

Review 01 is held in person and asks for two things:

  1. Technique and aesthetic precedents (3–5 slides) — the precedents detail how technique and aesthetic are used to define a coherent narrative/theme.

  2. Initial animation prototypes (draft resolution).

Lectures

  • Lecture 00 — Diorama — the course introduction, situating the diorama as a conceptual framework.
  • Lecture 01 — Time, Sequence, Distortion — the temporal and sequential techniques of the animated view.
