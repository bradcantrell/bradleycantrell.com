LAR 7416 · Digital Practices IV

Four workshops cover basic motion graphics topics in Adobe After Effects. The animation can be created directly in After Effects or may explore a hybrid solution that incorporates digital (3D animation, Rhino, 3ds Max, Maya) or physical models (stop-motion/time lapse). Beyond the workshops, students should explore their own techniques and methods — be curious.

Workshop 01 · After Effects Fundamentals

Adobe After Effects is a motion graphics and video editing software that integrates with the Adobe Creative Cloud suite of software. It is capable of animating attributes of assets in compositions in a 3D environment.

  • Projects / File Management — each AE project links to all of its asset files; keep a consistent structure (a folder containing all assets, with subfolders for each file or media type).
  • Projects & Compositions — compositions are contained in the project and hold assets on their timeline; assets can be used across multiple compositions.
  • Timeline & Keyframing — every layer property can be keyframed to create change between moments in the sequence.
  • Transforms & Effects — adjustment layers, effects (e.g. Tritone), and animated properties such as opacity.
  • Video Codecs / Render Sequence — add to the Render Queue, select the output module and H.264 codec, then render the video.

Workshop 02 · Illustrator & After Effects Workflow

Topics: masking, color correction and effects, compositions and pre-compose, motion tracker, 3D layers, and expressions.

  • Importing from Illustrator — bring files in as footage or as a composition; imported files remain linked, so edits to the Illustrator file can be reloaded (keep layer names unchanged).
  • Masking — masks crop everything outside a closed shape (or invert); mask feather and expansion soften or resize the edge; the shape can be keyframed.
  • Color correction — apply effects to a layer or an adjustment layer; all properties can be animated.
  • Motion tracking — track pixels within footage or stabilize footage; link layers to a null layer to adopt the tracked motion.
  • Expressions — link attributes or extend object behaviors (animate → add expression).
  • KeyLight — key out a color from footage, useful with green screen material.

Workshop 03 · 3D Layers & Transforms

Topics: 3D layers and transforms, renderers, cameras, and lights.

  • 3D layers — enable the 3D switch on a layer to add a third dimension to the transforms; content can be positioned, scaled, and rotated within 3D space.
  • Renderers — choose between the classic 3D renderer, Cinema 4D, and Ray Tracing; the latter two enable more options such as extruded linework and material options.
  • Cameras — create new views of the composition; all camera attributes, including position and target, can be animated.
  • Lights — lights interact with 3D layers through each layer’s material options; light properties can be animated like other objects.

Workshop 04 · Atmospheres, depth

The final workshop turns to atmospheres and depth — the environmental and spatial effects that bring the animated landscape view to life.
