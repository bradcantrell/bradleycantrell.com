Workshop 1 · Landforms + Water Flows

Aquatic Urban Systems

prototyping hydrological frameworks
Launch: Sept 1Review: Sept 19Geomorphology modeling table · Rhino + Grasshopper

Download the full Workshop 1 brief (PDF)

The focus of the first of three workshops is hydrology and topography as the genesis for a littoral urbanism. As the foundational beginning to the studio, students unpack topography and speculate on the behaviors of water in relation to terrain. It is necessary to imagine terrain as a thickness, a medium capable of supporting a range of urban and marine systems through managed saturation and flow. The workshop explores the relational processes, energies, and catalysts that give physical form to these rich littoral landscapes.

Working in teams of two, students develop multiple schemes that iteratively explore how topography can hold, distribute, and infiltrate water. Each iteration prototypes new instances that add complexity to the design research. Students are asked to consider not only the surface but to design the literal thickness of the system. Within each iteration there is operative potential to form dynamic material states — wet-dry, slow-fast, disturbed-stable.

Objectives

  1. To work iteratively through topographic studies that consider landscape processes
  2. Develop endemic and/or novel urban frameworks supported by the topographic and hydrological systems
  3. Explore an approach to topographic manipulation that considers material states within the terrain
  4. Consider time-based processes and the evolution of landscape within the framework of the site

Workshop Method

Using tools introduced in the pre-term workshop (Grasshopper and Rhino), students develop topographic models that explore the concept of hold, distribute, or infiltrate. First iterations explore a single term to generate multiple digital (1:1) and physical models (1:1000) using a variety of modeling methods, deploying varied topographic modules distributed across the site through deformation, scaling, and arrangement. Successive iterations explore the other two terms and layer multiple interactions (hold + infiltrate, hold + distribute, etc.), developing a classification method to create a genealogical tree of topographic forms.

After receiving a tutorial on the geomorphology modeling table, students create topographic modules that interact with sediment and water flow — an exploratory process where recording and observation are paramount. Software exports point clouds at different stages of recording, as well as image sequences of the interface operating, which become rough models for further exploration in Rhino and animations with overlays of annotation and diagrams. Physical explorations are further articulated with a sectional model (1:100) that advances and clarifies material relationships, and a refined 15″ × 15″ topographic model that examines form and materiality through etching and gray tones of Bristol, further illustrated through a graded site plan and section perspective.

Workshop Site

The area of exploration is a portion of the larger South Boston studio site — 300m × 450m (≈985′ × 1476′), picked from anywhere within the larger project area — providing a canvas to explore the relationship between topography, flow, and fluctuation. Each team develops comprehensive site research to understand the Boston waterfront and regional climate, framed to highlight a conceptual approach to the workshop, focusing on issues relating to hydrology, climate, and the physical landscape of Boston and the harbor.

Deliverables

  • Hydrologic timeline — based on site and contextual research, framing how water behaves on the site physically and temporally (quantity and duration).
  • Site research — multiple boards including diagrams of existing hydrology, soils, coastal change, topography, and bathymetry.
  • 1:10,000 contextual plan diagramming important regional systems.
  • Genealogical tree(s) — illustrating genealogical relationships of the topographic iterations, from Grasshopper models, physical modeling, precedents, and models extracted from the geomorphology modeling table.
  • 30-second looping video illustrating water flow (Grasshopper and Rhino), plus a 30-second looping video from footage extracted from the geomorphology modeling table.
  • 1:100 topographic modules for dispersal within the geomorphology modeling table, in materials that can be submerged in water for multiple physical simulations.
  • Section model @ 1:100 (30″ wide, 2–3″ deep) illustrating how the landscape holds, distributes, and infiltrates through the thickness and materiality of the ground.
  • Eight 7.5″ × 7.5″ physical models @ 1:1000 exploring iterations of the site topography.
  • 15″ × 15″ physical model @ 1:500 (Strathmore paper/pancake model, gray tones and etching), refining one iteration.
  • Topographic plan @ 1:500 (24″ × 36″) — a technical grading plan with layers of annotation illustrating how the landscape holds, infiltrates, and distributes water.
  • 15–30 serial sections @ 1:500 (36″ × 24″) with notation highlighting water and material conditions.
  • Section perspective @ 1:100 (24″ × 72″) — between technical documentation and illustration, focusing on materiality above and below ground.
