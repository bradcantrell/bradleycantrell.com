Week 02 · Observe · Articulate · Distill

site observation · inventory · modelling
July 22 – 26, 2019Sites: Riverview Park · The Downtown Mall

Download the full Week 02 brief (PDF)

“This drawing should be uncovering realities previously unseen or unimagined, rather than reproducing the obvious.”

James Corner — Week 02 brief

The second week focuses on site observation and inventory to develop a set of drawings and models that document a site's physical features and phenomena. Exercise 2.1 requires students in pairs to apply site observations to their AutoCAD base files; Exercise 2.2 develops an analogue plan through active methods of drawing and documenting; and Exercise 2.3 builds a “pancake” topographic model of the site. Representational tools are explored throughout the week to allow for a consistent and thoughtful study of the site.

Objectives

Actively apply the thinking and skills learnt in Week 01 on site. Through analogue diagrams, measured drawings and topographic modelling, gain an understanding of observational and notational methods for documenting, understanding and analyzing a site.

Exercises

Exercise 2.1 — Articulate: Building Complexity (Blindspots)

Update and articulate the AutoCAD base plan of your site. Each pair works on “blindspot” layers that add to the physical characteristics of the site — the data used to generate the initial plans are derived from remote sensing and general surveys, and the site visit reveals the sites to be much more complicated than the provided layers describe. What new layers are needed to further articulate the base plan?

Deliverables: one measured plan drawing per pair (36 × 48 in. bond paper). Review: Friday, July 26 — AutoCAD drawing plotted and pinned up.

Exercise 2.2 — Observe: Reading a Site

Each section visits one of two sites in Charlottesville, Virginia — Riverview Park or the Downtown Mall. During the visit, focus on attributes only attainable in person; pairs select at least two categories to document: infrastructure, vegetation, pathways + circulation, temporal (shadows, sounds, temperatures), or physical (habitats, dwellings, social). In pairs, expand this into a systematized analogue plan — not a literal plan of your site but an abstraction, drawing on a hybrid notational language carried forward from the Week 01 case studies.

Deliverables: one analogue drawing per pair (36 × 48 in. vellum). Review: Friday, July 26.

Exercise 2.3 — Distill: Modelling the Site

Each section collectively builds a topographic “pancake” model from 1/32 in. chipboard cut on the laser cutter. The chipboard layers are etched with contour elevation, building footprints, and roads; each site receives a title block with site name, north arrow, and graphic scale.

Deliverables: one chipboard topography model per section — two pieced-together models per site (1 ft = 1/64 in., 16 × 28 in.). Review: Friday, July 26.

Mid-Review

Students organize, plot, and pin up Exercise 2 for a cumulative mid-review of Weeks 1 and 2 with invited reviewers.

Workshops

  • Workshop 04 — Analogue Site Representation. Techniques and tools for building or drawing an analogue site representation.
  • Workshop 05 — Laser Cutting + AutoCAD. Setting up laser-cutting files and the pancake topographic model.
  • Workshop 06 — Introduction to FabLab. Fabrication shop tools and laser-cutters.
  • Workshop 07 — InDesign + Book Binding. Editing and curating design work — grids, margins, and editing styles to create a visual narrative.

Readings

  • Halprin, Lawrence. “Motation.” Process Architecture, July 1965, pp. 51–62.

Guest Lectures

Julie Bargmann — “Working with Site.” Internationally recognized designer of regenerative landscapes; founder of D.I.R.T. studio, whose work excavates the creative potential of degraded landscapes.

Thomas Nideroest — “When the Only Water Left is Gray.” Swiss landscape architect and researcher, synthesizing 2.5 years of design research on water scarcity and territory.
