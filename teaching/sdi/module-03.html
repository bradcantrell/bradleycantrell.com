Week 03 · Object · Translate

from unit to aggregation
July 29 – August 2, 2019Workshops: Rhino · 3D Printing · Grasshopper · Illustrator

Download the full Week 03 brief (PDF)

“Field conditions moves from the one toward the many: from individuals to collectives, from objects to fields.”

Stan Allen — “Field Conditions: from Object to Field”

Having completed a rigorous case study analysis and exercises in site representation, the third week focuses on the analysis and iterative translation of objects, re-presenting architectonic units and site objects. The first exercise involves the extraction and three-dimensional representation of units, or modules, from the case studies and site analysis. The second involves the iterative translation of these objects, from unit to aggregation. The final exercise is the siting of these studies. Varied representational methods will be tested, with an emphasis on thinking through making, and the production and curation of the physical artifact.

Objectives

  • Use constraints as a generative design tool.
  • Understand form, space and spatial sequence.
  • Practice an intensive and iterative design process.
  • Translate/synthesize content from the case study (Week 01) and site (Week 02).

Exercises

Exercise 3.1 — Extract: Identify / Represent Units

Revisit your work from the previous two weeks, identifying and drawing a catalog of spaces and objects from your case study and sitework. The unit — alternatively a module — is a singular element within a larger system of interconnected parts, with edges and thresholds that define its spatial arrangement.

3.1A — Case Study Units. Identify a minimum of three distinct modules from your case study, each explored diagrammatically as a volume with a z-axis in axonometric projection (45, 45, 90), then built as a 3D model in Rhino with a legible isometric view.

3.1B — Abstract Site Units. Identify three distinct site units from your Week 02 site analysis — trees/vegetation, landforms, site structure, or implied space — abstracting their geometries in Rhino to communicate their essential qualities.

Deliverables: Rhino models saved to the Classes folder; minimum three plates with isometric views pinned up. Due: Tuesday, July 31.

Exercise 3.2 — Translate: A Catalog of Operations from Unit to Aggregation

Working in teams of two, apply a rule-based design methodology through nine distinct operations, working with a series of constraints to state precisely what you would otherwise have only known intuitively. Identify individual spaces or objects from your case studies, combine them with those of your teammate, and shift them in form and scale to create new sequences and adjacencies of space for public use.

  1. Units — begin with your collective catalog of units from Exercise 3.1.
  2. Super-units — join modules to make 5 distinct super-units, each composed of 2–3 units and including at least one circulation unit (ramp or stairs); at least 2 joined on a vertical z-axis.
  3. Aggregation 1 — combine 10 instances of one super-unit, connected via existing apertures with a single joining strategy.
  4. Aggregation 2 — combine 5 instances of 2 super-units, connected via existing apertures or thresholds.
  5. Scale Up — aggregate 1 super-unit 3 times, or 3 different super-units once; scale one unit within a super-unit by 2x.
  6. Concept — choose one permutation with the most potential as a dynamic space for public occupation; determine a concept and how the intervention lands on the ground.
  7. Add, Subtract — if more than 70% is roofed, allow 30% open air; if less than 50% is roofed, add 30% coverage; between 50–70%, leave as designed.
  8. Material Translation — switch 50% of the most significant material into a contrasting material (heavy vs. light, permanent vs. temporal).
  9. Parametric Instancing — select 2–3 site objects with the most potential to enhance the negative/liminal space, testing placement through Grasshopper instancing, randomizing and culling.

Deliverables: Rhino models of progress saved to the Classes folder; a catalog of Operations 1–9, each printed to fit an 8 × 8 in. sheet; physical study models curated to show process. Due: Thursday, August 1.

Exercise 3.3 — Site: Synthesize and Situate Aggregations

Synthesize your work into a set of final representations, situating the complexities of form and space through drawings and models: a bird's-eye isometric projection (120, 120, 120) of your aggregation sited on a 5% slope, a section through the aggregation, and a 3D-printed model prepared for the LulzBots.

Due: Friday, August 2 — Week 3 pin-up.
