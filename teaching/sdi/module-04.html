Week 04 · System · Situate

landscape systems · GIS · the final proposal
August 5 – 9, 2019Final Review: August 10Site: Rivanna River corridor

Download the full Week 04 brief (PDF)

The final week introduces the concept of site selection and siting to synthesize the previous work and emphasize landscape systems. Students are introduced to ecological relationships through a series of lectures and exercises that form connections between site features and hydrological systems. Drawing from the previous week, students confront the full array of skills they have been taught to research, develop, and propose a design concept — completed via GIS mapping, diagramming, and digital modeling/drafting tools.

Objectives

  1. Develop a general understanding of Geographical Information Systems and cartography.
  2. Define water's role in the environment and how design can alter territorial landscapes.
  3. Understand the elements of a design process to produce a synthetic design proposal.
  4. Develop research around a specific design concept and apply it to multiple options.
  5. Create a site design proposal that integrates built form with landscape systems.

Exercises

Exercise 4.1a — Rivanna River Mapping

Working in pairs, assemble a GIS base map of the Rivanna River corridor through Charlottesville, Virginia — topography, roads, land cover, hydrology, and urban areas, plus 1–3 new layers of your choosing. Export the mappings to Adobe Illustrator to refine the linework and add annotations, creating a mapping that is aesthetically beautiful, rich in information, and coherent.

Deliverable: 36 × 36 in. print of the refined mapping — black and white with 1–2 accent colors, all vector linework.

Exercise 4.1b — Research: Site Action Definitions

Each pair is assigned a term (with its Oxford English Dictionary definition) and develops it into a design concept for a one-acre site, starting from a Week 03 aggregation. Express the term as a site action/force in six diagrams, and as a spatial condition in six more, developing each through the relation between built form and hydrology. Ask whether the definition is sufficient; what the etymology reveals; whether alternate definitions or other languages expand the term.

Deliverable: 12 diagrams, each 8 × 8 in.

The terms (definitions from the Oxford English Dictionary)

  • Catalyze — to increase the rate of (a reaction or process) by catalytic action; to produce by means of catalysis.
  • Resist — to stop or hinder; to act in opposition to (a force).
  • Transfer — to convey or take from one place, person, etc. to another; to transmit, transport.
  • (re)Enforce — to strengthen (a fortress) by extra works; to reinforce.
  • Evolve — to generate, develop, or modify by natural processes or gradual alteration.
  • Articulate — to express distinctly.
  • Transmogrify — to alter or change in form or appearance; to transform, metamorphose.
  • Transpose — to alter the order of (a set or series of things), or the position of (a thing) in a series.
  • Sequence — to arrange in a definite sequence or order.
  • Relate — to connect, to link; to establish a relation between.
  • Elucidate — to render lucid; to throw light upon, clear up, explain.
  • Integrate — to put or bring together (parts or elements) so as to form one whole.
  • Adapt — to make suitable or fit for a purpose, or conformable to specified conditions.
  • Cultivate — to grow and improve (a plant, crop, etc.), esp. for commercial purposes.
  • Accentuate — to make more noticeable or prominent; to emphasize.
  • Maintain — to keep up, preserve, cause to continue in being.
  • Trace — to pass along or over, tread (a path, way, street, etc.).
  • Carve — to cut up or subdivide.
  • Erode — to form (a channel, etc.) by gradual wearing away.
  • Deposit — to lay, put, or set down; to place in a more or less permanent position of rest.

Exercise 4.2 — Siting Objects and Terrain Manipulation

Within your assigned site area, identify two areas of approximately one acre suitable to deploy your concept. Using Rhino, develop three options for each site — manipulating the topography and evolving the aggregation to produce viable options that use your concept, considering hydrology, existing conditions, and access. Layout each option on a tabloid sheet with an isometric view and diagrammatic overlays that highlight how the site concept functions.

Deliverable: 6 isometric views with diagrammatic overlays on tabloid (11 × 17 in.) sheets.

Exercise 4.3 — Manipulate and Choreograph Site Systems

Refine a single option from Exercise 4.2 into a highly crafted site proposal, obsessing over the detail and craft of two primary representations: an isometric drawing and a section, section/elevation, or section/perspective through key site features.

Deliverable: 1 isometric drawing at 24 × 36 in.; 1 section or section/perspective; a refined and curated design process.

Final Review

For the final review you have 5 minutes to present your proposal: 1–2 minutes situating the project via previous work and research, 3–4 minutes explaining your concept and how it was achieved through the design proposal. Required: all Week 04 deliverables and the Week 03 Exercise 3.3 drawings, curated to pull from the previous three weeks where appropriate.
