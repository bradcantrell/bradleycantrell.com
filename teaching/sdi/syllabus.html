Introduction

Meeting Times: 9:00–18:00 daily, July 12 – August 10, 2019. Director: Bradley Cantrell, Chair and Professor of Landscape Architecture.

The Summer Design Institute (SDI) introduces a range of tools, skills and concepts rooted in architecture and landscape architecture to equip you with a foundation for further design work. These include spatial and material exploration, systems thinking, and site readings and response. Through this brief immersion in studio culture and practice, the intent is to gain a facility for seeing, thinking, and making critically. Essential to every aspect of this practice is drawing spatially in two and three dimensions. The projects will vacillate between longer explorations and quick studies; using careful, measured drawings in relation to the quick generation of alternatives. The methodologies introduced will emphasize design as an iterative process, rooted in thinking through the act of making.

Over the course of the month, you will be working both individually and collaboratively in groups. You will be asked to extract (week 1), distill (week 2), translate (week 3), and situate (week 4) as a way to understand key design principles and their relationship to context and use, and to reconfigure spatial and material languages for different sites. Each week's exercise will build upon one another conceptually and will be supported by a series of topical lectures, tutorials and workshops. The SDI will introduce the basics of design thinking and design vocabulary.

Course Objectives

The objectives of this course are to:

  1. Understand various types of spatial, physical and material operations in architecture and landscape architecture;
  2. Probe integral relationships and linkages between built form and its surrounding, between architecture and landscape architecture, between inside and outside;
  3. Practice creating architectural forms and understand its implication on multiscalar experience including body-scale;
  4. Establish a skillset to understand a site terrain spatially and to represent it 2- and 3-dimensionally;
  5. Situate design within a larger theoretical and historical context through case study research;
  6. Establish graphic literacy and precision for existing drawing conventions, understanding that each project/strategy may need its own language;
  7. Develop abstract and systems-based thinking and diagramming techniques;
  8. Value both digital and analog techniques for drawing and modeling and encourage their fluid and inventive combinations;
  9. Cultivate consistent and rigorous work habits that insist on critical thinking through iterative making;
  10. Learn how to prioritize, using time efficiently and productively in a design process;
  11. Explore your own design process and expand your personal toolbox for design;
  12. Craft narrative arguments and practice clear verbal and visual presentation.

Studio Practice

Sketchbook (Daily)

Use your sketchbook as a tool for recording and processing ideas and observations each day. The sketchbook represents your thought process, observations, and research visually through diagrams, sketches, and drawings. While some annotation should be present, the sketchbook for SDI is primarily a place for visual documentation.

Pinup Wall (Daily)

Keep your assigned pin-up wall up to date. At the end of every day of work, pin up the progress from that day — a daily curatorial process that includes scanning hand drawings, selectively printing digital drawings, or photos of models. These drawings form a tangible expression of how you use your time.

Design Log (Weekly)

Add to an ongoing digital design log by selecting key images from your daily wall and weekly work to compile a book of your work from the month. During the first and second week, collect work that illustrates your design process and outcomes; a workshop at the end of the second week introduces InDesign to create the book.

Readings (Weekly)

Readings are to be done in advance of the day they are discussed, or the week they are supporting — typically completed by Monday to inform your work for the week.

Reviews

Work is to be pinned up, with care, before the stated deadline. Use the narrative you are crafting with your design log to structure the visual and verbal presentation of your work. The entire duration of a review or pinup is mandatory to attend — the discussions of your classmates' work are often just as important to your own development.

Expectations

During studio time you are required to be working on studio projects at your desk. Drawings are to be done with care, whether by hand or digitally — trim paper to consistent sizes, keep awareness of drawing scales and north arrows, and cite any work shown that is not your own. You are expected to participate in all lectures and workshops, and for the entire duration of pinups and reviews. Assignments are to be completed and submitted by the time and day stated on the assignment. Collaboration and cross-fertilization are critical to strengthening ways of thinking and making as designers — plan to undertake your work in the studio rather than at home.
