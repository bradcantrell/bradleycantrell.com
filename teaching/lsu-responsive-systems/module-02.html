“The cyborg is a mechanism of deliberate transformation. It activates tactical operations in the environment in order to incite and/or simply control the transition between latent energy and flux.”

Cyborg Landscape — case study notes

In Phase 02, students continue to develop their competition proposals into a responsive architectural or landscape system. Participating in digitally focused workshops, students advance their design and computational skills by completing a series of tutorials and applying this knowledge to their projects. Responsive and interactive components and prototypes are developed through digital and physical models. Employing a bottom-up approach to design, these components and prototypes are developed into architectural and landscape systems — with each team determining the exact scale, scope, and programmatic implications of the project.

The phase proceeds through four assignments, moving from a parametric digital model toward a working physical prototype, and concludes by re-conceptualizing the original competition proposal in light of the prototype.

01 · Digital Prototype — due 10/7

In groups of 2–3 drawn from the original competition teams, develop a digital prototype of one of the responsive devices/systems in the original team proposal. The prototype uses Rhino to create the model and Grasshopper to drive the dynamic inputs — the sensors and their inputs that will drive responsive actions. The model should focus on the device's formal aesthetic and the transformations the device will make. Grasshopper components should drive actual values within the digital model, such as rotation (0–360 degrees), transformations (movement in feet/meters), and/or binary actions (off/on).

Deliverables: Rhino model, Grasshopper definition, and appropriate conceptual diagrams and models.

02 · Digital-Analog Prototype — due 10/17

Further developing the digital model, wire the inputs created in Grasshopper to the Firefly Uno Read component, driven by an Arduino and connected sensors. The sensor data will then directly control the digital device, creating a virtual prototype connected to environmental phenomena. The prototype will focus on 1–2 sensors that drive actions within the digital prototype, paying close attention to the interaction between the sensed data and how it is remapped to create desired responses. It may be necessary to create environments (models) that can be adjusted to provide the sensing field — a scaled model simulating the phenomenon being sensed, a constructed environment, or other devised methods.

03 · Digital-Analog Physical Prototype — due 11/04

After creating a responsive digital prototype, use the digital model and analog sensors to construct a physical prototype. The Firefly Uno Read will be connected to the Uno Write component to connect to the Arduino to drive servos, LEDs, or any other physical component necessary to develop the physical prototype. The physical prototype will expand the sensing and responses to create a fully articulated device that provides a proof of concept for a working device — orchestrating a range of sensing and responses that fully explore how the device might function within the environment, rather than exploring single interactions.

04 · Re-conceptualize Proposal — due 11/22

Each group re-examines the original team competition proposal and provides a revised scheme based on the developed prototype. The scheme is an evolution of the original proposal and may be developed in two ways:

  1. A revised team competition proposal — the original team creates a revised concept using each of the prototyped devices from the smaller groups, a unified approach presenting the original ideas and explaining the evolved proposal with consideration to how the prototypes have altered the approach.
  2. A divergent evolved proposal — each smaller group develops an evolved proposal that diverges from the original concept, focusing on an aspect of the original proposal and using the prototypes to explain how their approach has created a new or altered methodology.

Digital Environments

The phase relies on a range of platforms — Processing, Arduino, Rhino, Grasshopper, and Firefly — supported by in-studio tutorials and workshops.
