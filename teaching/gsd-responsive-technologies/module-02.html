Using the case study and onto-cartography (systems representation) from Project 01 as a starting point, the second project will propose how responsive technologies may play a role in the formulation, conception, or wholesale rethinking of contemporary infrastructure. The project is intentionally open-ended and asks each student to develop a strategy for speculation and/or prototyping that addresses the thesis of the seminar — that “responsive technologies and real-time sensing provide extended methodologies to resist, choreograph, curate, and manage complex ecological relationships.”

The second phase will be an iterative exploration of new riverine/coastal infrastructures and the connection between sensing, monitoring, and feedback. The projects will focus on the relationship between sensing, monitoring, and response. An essential question must be explored as the prototypes evolve — a question surrounding the role of humanity in the manipulation of ecological systems. The interaction between responsive infrastructure and the ecological systems that are slowly integrated will question our perception of ecological systems as we understand the aesthetic, performative, and systemic qualities of ecology as a curated medium.

The view of infrastructure as a modulator, disrupting and engaging surrounding systems, forms a relationship of opposition and/or scarcity. How does infrastructure interrupt larger systems? What can it contribute? How does the infrastructural prototype function as a node within a large network?

Approach

This work should engage hybrid approaches that oscillate between analog and digital techniques of modeling or simulation. The project should be connected to an evolving sensing mechanism(s) and may engage coastal/riverine conditions from the micro to macro, local to global. This form of design research engages speculation and prototyping that focuses on sensing, monitoring, and actuation. The design of the design speculations and/or prototypes can take on many forms, both physical and digital, but should fundamentally address a need to test a symbiosis with dynamic processes in relation to site and time.
