Introduction

The interface between the constructed environment and ecological systems requires a blurring of strategies to address nascent forms of urbanism, biological engineering, and technological interface. These strategies encourage the application of responsive technologies, to form new relationships through sensing and feedback, and therefore developing novel ecological systems. These novel forms of ecology demand a deconstruction of current forms of settlement, infrastructure, and biological systems to understand the constructed landscape as a synthesizer of biotic and abiotic processes. The interstices of these new relationships become the medium in which the course examines new potentials for sensing, monitoring, and automation as a starting point to propose new hydrological, coastal, and riverine infrastructures. Responsive Technologies in Landscape Architecture examines the role of nascent technologies that promote active and real-time methods of environmental management. These methodologies span a range of scales, from micro adjustments within processes to regional management and monitoring to global remote sensing systems. Primarily, responsive technologies create an applied, recursive or iterative relationship between computation and biology.

The course focuses on a primary method of inquiry that unpacks coastal and riverine infrastructures through the lens of what Levi Bryant calls, an alien phenomenology. This refers to a mode of sensing through objects (machines) and identifies the connections between these actors. The identified connections will be the framework for an onto-cartography, a mapping of the interconnections between multiple actors performing in nested and networked relationships. The resultant framework, the onto-cartography, will be continually referenced and modified to test or speculate upon methods of sensing and feedback through media, mockups, and/or prototypes.

Participants will be expected to develop a precise media and/or prototyping strategy to interrogate the potential of responsive systems in the development of infrastructural proposals for the assigned sites. It is necessary that all students have developed a repertoire of representation, prototyping, and modeling skills as the pedagogy expects students to engage media and technology to develop nascent approaches to site design, ecological management, and infrastructure.

Format

The format for the course is a research seminar in which instruction requires participation, discussion, prototyping, and representation. Lectures, readings, and discussions will provide a broad framework to experiment with responsive technologies, robotics, machine intelligence, and simulation to imagine new forms of coastal and riverine infrastructure.

Prerequisites

While some software/hardware instruction will be available, the course is not intended as a software workshop. Students will be asked to engage the topic through their own expertise in representation, programming, and making and therefore requires individuals to have an inherent interest in these topics. The course is open to all GSD students with a mix of landscape architects, architects, urban designers, MDes, and DDes students desired.
