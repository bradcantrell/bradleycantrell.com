Infrastructural Case Study

watershed research · onto-cartography
Introduced: Jan 30Watershed due: Feb 13Review: Mar 20

Download the full Project 01 brief (PDF)

The formation of contemporary watersheds is a synthesis of the geomorphology, climate, biological systems, and anthropogenic influences. To develop a more comprehensive understanding of these systems the first project will closely examine the anthropogenic change within the context of the watershed through the creation of a case study. The case study will serve as the central research topic and medium for speculation throughout the semester. All the work should be cited throughout the project — this includes data sources, acquisition of imagery, collecting of historical maps, and proper citations of literature.

Watershed Scale

Working in five-person teams, choose one person to be the “watershed expert” of your team — work together to share resources and split up tasks to find relevant infrastructure, policy, and socio-cultural perceptions.

  1. Mississippi River Watershed
  2. Han River Watershed and Four Major Rivers Restoration Project
  3. Yangtze River Watershed
  4. Mekong River and Delta Watershed

To begin the watershed scale research the team should examine these topics among others chosen by the team:

  • Historic Hydrologic System — collection and re-drawing of historic mappings; ecology
  • Physical Features — geomorphology, topography, vegetation/biota, hydrology
  • Policy and Major Alterations — timeline and diagrammatic mappings
  • Risk — risk to settlement/flooding, environmental risk, combined settlement and environmental risk
  • Socio-cultural influences — human–water dynamics: how social and cultural perceptions of hydrologic systems have shaped the watershed, and how the hydrologic systems have in turn shaped the societies within the watershed

Focused Research

Using the watershed research, develop a focused case study that examines a specific policy, physical structure, or socio-cultural perception within the region. The case study should be a deep dive that develops a comprehensive look at the topic itself as well as connecting the topic back to the systems within the watershed. The examples below are to help each team get started; it is also possible to propose alternatives. Develop a relationship between the projects with other group members — the case studies should have some relationship and overarching theme. The “watershed expert” will develop a body of research that synthesizes these relationships.

For this stage of the research each team member will explore the creation of their case study through the following:

  • Timeline — situate with other technologies, practices, phasing/development (can be done as a group); planning documents, envisioned and enacted
  • Collection of marketing/propaganda/writings/rhetoric — discussing how the infrastructure was perceived; political climate and context; cultural overview
  • Interface with surrounding systems — soft/hard connections, permanent/flexible
  • Engineering diagram — highlights the performance of the infrastructure (Mississippi River plumbing diagram precedent)
  • Ecological and Territorial Influence — connections with other systems; infrastructural model

Watershed Foci

Mississippi River Watershed (United States)

  • Policy — 2017 Coastal Louisiana Master Plan (Coastal Protection Restoration Authority, SONRIS data portal); Flood Control Act / Levees Only Policy / Swamp Lands Act
  • Physical — Old River Control Structure, Bonnet Carre Spillway, Morganza Spillway, Levee System, Lake Borgne Surge Barrier, Caernarvon Diversion, Casting Fields
  • Maintenance / Management / Influence — dredging, stabilization, nutrient overload
  • Socio-cultural Perceptions

Four Major Rivers Project (South Korea)

  • Policy — Green Growth / Green New Deal
  • Physical — river diversions, dams, levees, hydro power, floodplains
  • Maintenance / Management / Influence — dredging, stabilization
  • Socio-cultural Perceptions

Yangtze River (China)

  • Policy — China Three Gorges Project Corporation, resettlement
  • Physical — river diversions, dams, levees, hydro power, floodplains
  • Maintenance / Management / Influence — dredging, stabilization
  • Socio-cultural Perceptions

Mekong Delta (Vietnam + others)

  • Policy — shared resource (China, Myanmar, Lao PDR, Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam), resettlement
  • Physical — river diversions, dams, levees, hydro power, floodplains
  • Maintenance / Management / Influence — dredging, stabilization
  • Socio-cultural Perceptions
