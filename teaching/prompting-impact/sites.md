# Knoxville Sites — Living Systems & Ecological Intelligence

Seven sites across Knoxville. Pick one. Each has a hook (the ecological question) and the live data that speaks to it. Coordinates are approximate; refine them to your specific parcel.

## 1. Tennessee River at Knoxville (Fort Loudoun Lake)
- **Question:** how does a dammed river behave as a managed system — and what does "real-time" even mean for a reservoir?
- **Data:** NWPS live stage + flow + forecast (`knot1`), TVA lake levels / releases, NWS flood stages.
- **Hook:** the river is both a natural system and a piece of infrastructure; the gage tells you what it's doing *right now*.

## 2. Third Creek
- **Question:** how does an urban creek flood, and what does the impervious surface upstream do to it?
- **Data:** no live gauge (ungauged) — use rainfall (Open-Meteo/ACIS) + a design storm (NOAA Atlas 14 → runoff → inundation); NLCD land cover, weather.
- **Hook:** the classic urban-watershed assessment — runoff, flash, water quality.

## 3. The Urban Wilderness (South Knoxville)
- **Question:** what lives in a reclaimed post-industrial landscape, and how is succession playing out?
- **Data:** iNaturalist observations (radius around the site), tree canopy, land cover.
- **Hook:** a neo-wild in the middle of the city — species data tells you what's actually there vs. what you assume.

## 4. Downtown / UT Campus
- **Question:** where is the urban heat island, and how bad is the air?
- **Data:** OpenAQ (PM2.5/O₃), Open-Meteo temperature, tree canopy.
- **Hook:** the most legible "environmental consequence" for a dense core — heat and air quality.

## 5. Sharp's Ridge / House Mountain
- **Question:** what happens at the forest edge between the city and the Smokies foothills?
- **Data:** iNaturalist (biodiversity), land cover, elevation.
- **Hook:** the gradient from urban to wild — species and cover shift sharply here.

## 6. First Creek (daylighting candidate)
- **Question:** what would it mean to bring a buried creek back to the surface?
- **Data:** ungauged — design storm (NOAA Atlas 14 → runoff → inundation); land cover, elevation; then a *proposed intervention* (your design move).
- **Hook:** the "what-if" — read the system, then propose the restoration.

## 7. Oak Ridge / DOE Reservation
- **Question:** how does a landscape shaped by decades of industrial/environmental legacy read today?
- **Data:** air + water monitoring, land cover, remediation history.
- **Hook:** a true neo-wild — a landscape whose ecology is inseparable from its human/environmental history.

## Choosing a site

Pick the one you can *actually visit or verify* — the assessment is only as honest as the data you can point to. You will use the `ecological-assessment` skill and `map-template.html` to build a live map and write the assessment.
