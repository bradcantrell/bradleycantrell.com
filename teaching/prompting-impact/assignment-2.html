❯ cat brief.mdthe premise

Assignment 1 put one reach in front of two instruments. A model read it from four coordinate pairs, working from everything it had read and nothing it had seen. You then read the same reach from an aerial photograph with your own eyes. Where the two agreed, you learned something about the place. Where they parted, you learned something about the instruments.

That is the beginning of analysis rather than the end of it. A reach is never described by a single resource, and imagery is only the first. Historical photographs, a gauge standing in the water, more than eighty years of reanalysis weather for the cell nearest you, land cover, species records, and the bank under your own feet each hold something the others do not. None of them alone will answer the question you posed.

Assignment 2 assembles the panel. You are building a map of your reach that carries live data, historical data, and an accounting of where every number on it came from.

The panel also has to hold its sources in tension. When two of them disagree about your reach, the disagreement is not a problem to settle by choosing one. It is evidence of something the reach is doing that neither source was built to see. This assignment asks you to keep those disagreements on the page and to say what they reveal.

❯ step 0 · the operatorhow to work with the model

You will build this with a language model, and that is the intended way to build it. The model is the instrument you are learning to play. It is not the source of the answer, and you are the one who decides what counts. Every step below names what to hand it and what to ask for back.

Three rules hold across every step.

1
Paste the file
Describe nothing from memory. If the question is about your bounds, paste the KML. A model that cannot read your file will invent numbers that look exactly as plausible as the right ones.
2
Ask for the file, not the values
Coordinates retyped from a number on a screen are where this assignment goes wrong. Have the model write the file, then download it.
3
Make it show its source
Ask which endpoint, which archive, which photograph. A layer with no source behind it cannot go in the ledger, and the ledger is the assignment.

The ledger takes three forms here, the marks on your dashboard, the kinds in your meta.json and your source-assessment.md.

If you are working in a chat window that cannot write files, ask for the contents in a code block and save the file yourself.

Your KML is already in your Session 2 folder. It is named site-NN-your-name.kml, and it is the file this assignment means every time it says your KML. Paste its text, or attach the .kml if your interface takes uploads. Do not use the KMZ from Session 1. That is a zipped copy of the same file, and a zipped file cannot be pasted.
❯ step 1 · the questionone sentence

Go back to the one question you wrote in Assignment 1. If the aerial changed it, use the new version. Write it at the top of your page, above everything else. The dashboard starter you open in step 3 has a Question panel for it, and a line for what would prove you wrong.

The map exists to answer that question. This is the test of whether a layer of information belongs on it. A layer that does not help answer your question is ornament. You may not know at first which layers matter, so try a few and keep the ones that change how you read the reach.

Say what would change your mind. Below your question, write one sentence naming the measurement that would prove you wrong. If nothing could prove you wrong, the question is not yet a question.
Feed the modelPaste your Assignment 1 model file, both turns, and the sentence you wrote. Ask it to test the sentence against the sources in step 2, and tell you which of them could prove it wrong.
❯ step 2 · gather the signalfour kinds of evidence

Look widely, be critical, and use only what your question needs. Every source below was queried and returned data on November 9. They are grouped by what kind of evidence they are, and the kind is what your ledger is built from. The query for every source, and what each one will try to trick you with, is on the data sources page.

kindsourceswhat they give you
measured NWPS gauge knot1 Stage, discharge, forecast, flood categories. An instrument on the river near Knoxville, maintained by an agency that answers for it. Action 817 ft, minor 820 ft, moderate 825 ft, major 830 ft.
modeled Open-Meteo forecast, air quality, flood. NLCD land cover. FEMA flood zones. USGS 3DEP elevation. A documented method applied to data. Excellent at the scale it was built for, and carrying every assumption of that method.
observed iNaturalist Species recorded inside your quad. Query your bounding box, then keep only the points inside your four corners. A radius search reaches far beyond your quad. A record is where somebody with a phone happened to walk.
archival Open-Meteo archive, 1940 to present. McClung Digital Collection. Planetary Computer scenes. Reanalysis weather back to 1940 for the grid cell nearest your quad, and a century of photographs of this river. A reanalysis is a model run over the past rather than a station record, and it returns the cell and not your point. Treat it as evidence rather than as a measurement.

Step 5 accepts two more kinds than the four above. Mine covers the layer you make yourself, and asserted covers a claim a model produced with nothing behind it. Four kinds to gather with, six to file under.

The live river gauge is not the one you expect. Five USGS Tennessee River sites sit inside Knox County and none of them serves live data. The river here is a regulated TVA reservoir reach, so USGS returns an empty array and the API looks broken. The live instrument is the NWPS gauge, not USGS. Start with knot1.
Required · the gap two numbers, one river

Two answers for the flow of the same river exist in this set. The NWPS gauge reports 55.3 kcfs, about 1,566 m³/s, measured at a dam-regulated reach. The Open-Meteo flood model, queried at a point on the channel near downtown, reported 266 m³/s.

Your two numbers will not be these. The flood model answers for a cell about 5 km across, so what it returns depends on which cell your point falls in. Its river does not always sit where your aerial shows it, and a cell off its river returns almost nothing. So ask it at a point on your channel, your center and your four corners, and keep the cell that returns the most water. The dashboard starter does this for you. The response names the cell it answered for. Write that down beside the number, and mark the cell's center on your map as the model's cell, never as your quad. One test quad came out at 5.7×, another at 18.2×. Neither student had broken anything.

Neither number is a lie, and neither one is the river. The flood model is a good instrument answering a different question. It does not represent how TVA operates these reservoirs, so it describes a river that is not managed the way this one is. The gauge reports the river as it is actually run.

That makes the gap a reading in its own right. More than one explanation fits it. The model knows nothing of the dams. Its grid cell may not sit on the main channel. It answers for a day while the gauge answers for an hour. Each of those would mean something different about your reach, and you do not yet know which one, or which mix, is true.

Put both numbers on your dashboard, side by side, with the ratio between them, and do not let the page pick a winner. The starter already does this. Then write the paragraph, which goes in your source-assessment.md under Where the sources disagree. Not which number is right, but what the gap shows you about this river, which form of divergence it is (step 6), and what you would watch to tell the explanations apart. This is the clearest lesson in this corridor and you already hold both ends of it.

Feed the modelPaste the queries from the data sources page and your KML. Ask it to fetch each source and write the response into data/, not to describe what the response would probably say. Anything it did not just fetch is asserted.
❯ step 3 · build the instrumentthree panels

Start from dashboard-starter.html. It already queries the gauge, the weather, the air quality, the flood model and iNaturalist, and it already computes the measured against modeled ratio. Open it, read it, save your copy as index.html, and make it yours. Its layers count toward the assignment, so you do not rebuild them.

What the map needs. The starter's layers, one more from the data sources page that helps answer your question, and one layer of your own. That is the whole requirement. Choose the extra layer by what your question needs, not by what is easiest to add. A second extra layer, and anything beyond it, is optional, and the end of this page says how. There is a finished example on a spare reach that no one was assigned. Look at it to see the standard, not the answer. Your question and your layer should look nothing like it.

A dashboard has three jobs and they are not the same job.

1
Instrument panel
Live numbers that refresh. Stage, discharge, temperature, PM2.5, AQI. If nothing on your page changes on its own, you have made a poster.
2
Layer controls
Toggles for what you actually built. Species points, flood zones, land cover, elevation, historic photographs. Each layer answers a different question or it does not earn a toggle.
3
The ledger
Beside the live numbers, show the modeled ones and put a visible mark on every claim that came from a language model without a source. Where two sources disagree, show both and the gap between them, never one number standing in for the pair. The page should show its own uncertainty rather than hide it.
One layer has to be yours. It does not have to come from standing on the ground. Getting to your site in person is optional, and some of these reaches cannot be reached in the time you have. What is not optional is that one layer comes out of your own hand and your own reading rather than out of an endpoint.

If you are not sure what to make, use the default. Open your aerial in geojson.io, trace the edge of the water along the bank of your quad as a line of about twenty points, and add three properties, what you traced, how, and the date of the imagery. Save it as GeoJSON into data/. That is about forty minutes, and it is a real reading, because the edge you see is not the edge any dataset draws.

Anything else below is welcome instead. Draw a feature off the imagery in a drawing tool and export it as a layer. Count something across the whole quad and record the count and the method. Cut a section through the corridor from elevation data and draw what it shows you. Read a 1930s photograph closely and map the thing it holds that the current aerial does not. If you can get to the site, walk it and photograph what you find.

Every API on this list can be called by anyone in ninety seconds, so a page assembled entirely from them says nothing about your reach that a page about any other reach would not also say. The layer you make is what turns the map into an argument about this 2 km.
Then set your layer against an instrument. Find one endpoint that claims to describe the same thing your layer does. The bank you traced against the NLCD class for those pixels. Your count against what iNaturalist records in the quad. The 1930s photograph against the current aerial. Where your reading and the instrument part ways is the most specific thing your page knows about this reach. Leave both on the map. This is the second disagreement you write up in step 6.
Feed the modelPaste dashboard-starter.html and your one sentence. Ask for one layer at a time and ask which lines it touched. A model that rewrites the whole file leaves you unable to see what broke.
❯ step 4 · host itpublic url

Your map has to live at a real URL. A file on your laptop cannot be assembled with seventeen other maps.

Host on Netlify. It takes a folder, needs no repository, and lets you name the address by site number.

1
Drop it
Put index.html and your data/ folder together in one folder. Drag that folder onto app.netlify.com/drop.
2
Claim it
Sign up or sign in, which is free, and claim the site. A drop you leave unclaimed sits behind a temporary password and is deleted, so it will not be live.
3
Rename it
In the site's configuration, change the site name to your site number and a word or two, such as site-07-river-bend. Names must be unique, so site-07 alone may be taken. Your address becomes https://site-07-river-bend.netlify.app.
4
Update it
To change the map later, open the site's Deploys page and drag the folder there again. The address stays the same, which matters, because Assignment 3 builds on this map at this address.

If you already publish with GitHub Pages and your username is not your name, you may use it instead.

Test it in a private window while logged out. If it only works when you are signed in, it does not work.

Name it by site number, not by your name. Use site-NN in the site name and leave your name off the page title. The map is public and permanent, and there is no reason for a public page to carry your identity.
It has to be https at a real address. The corridor map, your classmates and I read your map there. Prototype locally if you like, but the version you hand in must be served.
Feed the modelAsk for the exact clicks or commands for your host, one stage at a time, and paste back any error text exactly as it appeared. A paraphrased error sends the model after the wrong problem.
❯ step 5 · the handshakemeta.json

Do not type this file by hand. Every coordinate in it should arrive from a file the model can read, never from a number you copied off a screen. Paste the example above, paste your KML from Session 2, and ask for meta.json. The only values you supply yourself are the ones in the block marked mine to fill in.

Read the KML pasted below and write meta.json for my corridor map.

  mine to fill in
  site number    NN
  name           my name
  hosted url     https://...
  one question   my sentence from step 1
  my layers      listed below, with whatever I know about each source

  KML
  <paste the text of site-NN-your-name.kml here>

Then check three things before you trust the result. bounds is the smallest longitude and latitude first and the largest second, and the box holds all four corners. center is longitude before latitude, and it sits inside the box. corners has four pairs, not five. These catch people working from memory, and a model will only get them right if it is reading your file rather than guessing.

kind is what lets the corridor map color you honestly. Measured is a number from an instrument. Modeled is a number from a method. Observed is a person seeing a thing. Archival is evidence from the past. Mine is the layer you gathered yourself. If a value came from a language model with nothing behind it, say asserted and do not dress it up. A map that admits one weak layer is more trustworthy than one that hides it.

❯ step 6 · the ledgersource quality

This is the part being assessed. Not whether your data is verified. Whether you can say what each source is good for, and which of your claims it can actually hold up. Write it in source-assessment.md, the template for this step.

Quality belongs to a pairing, a source and a claim about a specific 2 km reach. The same global flood model is excellent for screening a continent and useless for telling you what the water is doing at your quad. The judgment is always relative to the question. Kind says how a number was made. Tier says who answers for it.

Tiers of authority five
1
Instrumented by an agency that answers for it
The NWPS gauge. NWS warnings. A physical instrument, maintained by an organization that is accountable when it fails.
2
Modeled or derived, with a stated method
Open-Meteo, NLCD, FEMA flood zones. The assumptions of the method are usually the thing that matters.
3
Volunteer and crowdsourced
iNaturalist. Enormously valuable and unevenly distributed.
4
Archival and historical
McClung photographs, old maps, engineering records. A 1920 photograph is a real observation by a real observer, and it is one frame, chosen by someone, at one moment.
5
Machine assertion with no source
Anything a language model told you that you could not trace. Useful as a hypothesis. It cannot support a claim about the world.

Your own layer has no tier. Write mine in the tier column, the way the example does, and say in the method column how you made it.

Six dimensions in your table and your prose

Authority. Who produced it, under what mandate, and do they answer for errors. Method. Measured, modeled, derived, observed, or recalled. Most disagreements between sources are method disagreements wearing a numbers costume. Resolution. Grain against your quad, and which axis fails. Latency. Realtime, hourly, daily, annual, or frozen in 2019. Fitness. Can it answer what you asked of this reach, or does it answer a neighboring question at a different scale. Stated error. Is an uncertainty given, or is the number bare. A source that publishes its error bars is easier to trust than one that does not, even when the error is large. Five of them are in your table, with authority as the tier column. Stated error goes in your prose.

The weakest link rule. A claim inherits the quality of the weakest source in the chain that produced it. If you write that your reach sits at 811.6 ft, and that number came from a gauge four kilometers upstream in a different hydraulic reach, your claim is only as strong as that spatial transfer and not as strong as the gauge. The instrument is excellent. The transfer is not. Do this to three of your own sentences.
Feed the modelPaste your meta.json and ask it to argue for the weakest layer, not the strongest. What a source is good for is something it can find. What your claim needs is yours to decide.
Holding the tension divergence as knowledge

The weakest link rule tells you how far a single claim can be trusted. It does not settle a disagreement. When two sources give incompatible accounts of the same thing, the gap between them is information about what your reach is doing that neither can capture alone. Disagreements come in three forms.

1
Calibration
The same variable read at different resolutions. A point gauge against a gridded flood model. A reanalysis cell against your own coordinates. The mismatch tells you the scale at which the process is actually working.
2
Modality
Your own reading and a technical source register different dimensions of one thing. The bank you traced against a land cover class. A photograph against a species list. The gap between them is the thing's full complexity.
3
Intentional
Sources built by institutions with different mandates answer different questions. A FEMA flood zone is drawn for insurance. An NWS flood category is drawn for warning. Put them side by side and you see what each was built to see.
Do not choose, and do not average. When two sources disagree about the same claim, record both readings, name the form of divergence, say what the gap suggests your reach is doing, and say what would test it. A disagreement you can describe is worth more than an agreement you manufactured.
Feed the modelPaste the two readings that disagree and ask not which is right but what each could be seeing that the other cannot. Ask for three explanations and for the evidence that would tell them apart. Ask it for the disagreement, not the verdict.

❯ cat hard.mdthe trap

The temptation is to rank the sources once and reuse the ranking.

The gauge is the best source in your set and it cannot tell you anything specific about your quad. iNaturalist is the weakest by method and it is the only source that knows a species is actually there. A 1920 photograph has no method at all and it is the only thing on your page that shows what this bank looked like before the dam.

Quality is a fit between a source and a question rather than a standing rank, and the whole skill is being honest about which fit you have.

The second temptation runs the other way. Once two sources disagree, it feels like rigor to pick the better one and move on. The ranking tells you how much weight each source can carry. It does not tell you what their disagreement means, and the disagreement is often the most specific thing your page knows about this reach. Keep the tension on the page and look for productive ways to interpret it.

Use whichever model you like to build this. It does not go in the ledger. Only the source of the number does.

❯ cat more.mdoptional

If you have the time and want to go further, add more layers. Each one still has to earn its toggle by helping answer your question, and each goes in your ledger and in meta.json with its kind.

Some worth trying, all on the data sources page. FEMA flood zones. NLCD land cover. A section across the corridor from 3DEP elevation. The weather archive since 1940 for your cell. A McClung photograph placed on your map. NWS alerts. A historic Landsat scene.

Nothing here counts against you if you leave it out. A map with three honest layers is better than one with eight that answer nothing.
