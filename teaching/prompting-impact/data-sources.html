Data Sources

Every query below was run against a real quad on November 10
❯ cat how-to-use.mdpaste this page

This is the endpoint list step 2 asks you to hand the model. Copy the queries below, paste them with your KML and your meta.json, and ask the model to fill in your values and fetch each one into data/. None of them needs a key.

The placeholders are always the same. {lat}, {lon} is your quad's center from the KML. {west}, {south}, {east}, {north} is your bounding box from meta.json. {chan_lat}, {chan_lon} is a point on the main channel of the river inside your quad, which you pick off the aerial.

Your quad is rotated. It follows the river, so its bounding box is about twice its area. Anything you query by box, species above all, has to be clipped back to the four corners of your quad before it means anything about your quad.

NWPS gauge knot1measured · tier 1

Stage, discharge, forecast and flood categories for the Tennessee River at Knoxville. Action 817 ft, minor 820, moderate 825, major 830. It is one point in the water, so say how far it is from your quad.

https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/knot1

NWS alertsmeasured · tier 1

Active watches and warnings for Tennessee, as polygons and text. Most days it is empty for your quad, and an empty answer is a real reading.

https://api.weather.gov/alerts/active?area=TN

Open-Meteo flood modelmodeled · tier 2

Daily river discharge from GloFAS, a global model that works on cells about 5 km across. Its river does not always sit where your aerial shows it. The model's river network is coarse, so the cell carrying the main river can be a whole cell away from the real channel, and a cell off its river returns almost nothing. At one quad the center returned 0.37 m³/s and the channel 269. At another the channel returned 0.13 and the center 287. So ask at your channel point, your center and your four corners, and keep the cell that returns the most water, usually hundreds of m³/s. The response gives the latitude and longitude of the cell it answered for. Record that pair. The dashboard starter does all of this for you. The API returns no outline for the cell, so mark its center and label it as the model's cell, never as your quad.

https://flood-api.open-meteo.com/v1/flood?latitude={chan_lat}&longitude={chan_lon}&daily=river_discharge&forecast_days=1
then the same with {lat},{lon} and each of your four corners

iNaturalistobserved · tier 3

Query your bounding box, then keep only the observations inside your four corners. Do not use a radius. A 25 km radius returns records from all over Knoxville, and in one test none of the fifty newest fell inside the quad. Two hundred records is about 7.5 MB, so save the file once rather than fetching it on every page load if your map gets slow.

https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/observations?swlat={south}&swlng={west}&nelat={north}&nelng={east}&per_page=200&order=desc&order_by=observed_on
❯ groundland, water, elevation

FEMA flood zones (NFHL)modeled · tier 2

Use the image export as a map overlay. It answers in a few seconds. Feature queries over a whole quad often time out or reset, so only query a small area when you need attributes, and leave out the geometry. A value of −9999 means no value was published. It is not an elevation.

overlay  https://hazards.fema.gov/arcgis/rest/services/public/NFHL/MapServer/export?bbox={west},{south},{east},{north}&bboxSR=4326&imageSR=4326&size=900,900&layers=show:28&transparent=true&format=png32&f=image
zones    https://hazards.fema.gov/arcgis/rest/services/public/NFHL/MapServer/28/query?geometry={a small box}&geometryType=esriGeometryEnvelope&inSR=4326&spatialRel=esriSpatialRelIntersects&outFields=FLD_ZONE,ZONE_SUBTY&returnGeometry=false&f=json

USGS 3DEP elevationderived · tier 2

One point per request, in feet, about four seconds each, and roughly one request in five fails and has to be retried. Keep a section to fifteen points or so. Over water the model is noisy by several feet, which can be larger than the difference you want to claim. The gauge reports in feet above sea level, so compare like with like.

https://epqs.nationalmap.gov/v1/json?x={lon}&y={lat}&units=Feet&wkid=4326

NLCD 2021 land covermodeled · tier 2

A 30 m national classification from one year. Show it as a map layer, and ask for the class at a single point when you want to compare it with your own reading.

layer    https://www.mrlc.gov/geoserver/mrlc_display/wms   layers=NLCD_2021_Land_Cover_L48
a point  https://www.mrlc.gov/geoserver/mrlc_display/wms?service=WMS&version=1.1.1&request=GetFeatureInfo&layers=NLCD_2021_Land_Cover_L48&query_layers=NLCD_2021_Land_Cover_L48&bbox={lon-0.0005},{lat-0.0005},{lon+0.0005},{lat+0.0005}&srs=EPSG:4326&width=11&height=11&x=5&y=5&info_format=application/json
❯ pastarchival

Open-Meteo archive, 1940 to presentarchival · tier 4

Reanalysis, which is a model run over the past, for the grid cell nearest your center. The response names the cell. Treat it as evidence for that cell, not as a record at your point.

https://archive-api.open-meteo.com/v1/archive?latitude={lat}&longitude={lon}&start_date=1940-01-01&end_date=2025-12-31&daily=precipitation_sum,temperature_2m_max&timezone=America/New_York

McClung Digital Collectionarchival · tier 4

Historic photographs of Knoxville. The records have no coordinates, so finding a photograph of your reach is real search work. Search for a landmark, a road or a bridge you can see in your quad, then place the photograph on your map yourself, which makes its location your reading.

https://cmdc.knoxlib.org/digital/api/search/searchterm/{a place name}/maxRecords/50

Planetary Computer scenesarchival · optional

Landsat and Sentinel scenes back to the 1980s. Finding scenes is easy. Displaying one needs a signed asset link, which is more setup than most maps need this week. Use it only if you want a historical image the McClung collection does not have.

POST https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/api/stac/v1/search
{"collections":["landsat-c2-l2"],"bbox":[{west},{south},{east},{north}],"datetime":"1984-01-01/1990-12-31","limit":5}
