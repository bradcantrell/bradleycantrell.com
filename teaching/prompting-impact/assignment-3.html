Prompting Impact · Module D · Assignment 3

The Score

Due Monday, November 16 · 2200 EST
❯ cat brief.mdthe premise

Assignment 1 read your reach. Assignment 2 sensed it, and taught you to keep the disagreements between your sources on the page instead of settling them. Both were about knowing. This one is about daring something.

You will make a proposition for your 2 km, and it will not be a plan. A plan draws the finished thing and asks the river to hold still while it is built. This river has never held still. Lawrence Halprin wrote scores instead, instructions that set up a performance and leave room for the performers to do something no one drew in advance. Your performers are the water, the sediment, the species, the TVA release schedule and the people who use this bank. You are writing the rules they play by.

Assignment 3 writes the score, has the machine perform it, and connects it to your neighbors. Eighteen scores on one river make a single corridor proposition, and the corridor map will show where they reinforce each other and where they collide.

❯ step 0 · the operatorthe machine as co-creator

In the first two assignments the model was an instrument you audited. Here it is also a collaborator that generates options, more and stranger than you would draw yourself, including images. You still direct it, and you still audit every contribution. The ledger does not stop at the data. It runs through the design.

The three rules from Assignment 2 still hold. Paste the file. Ask for the file, not the values. Make it show its source. Two more apply here.

4
Ask for the version you would never draw
The first answer a model gives is the most probable one, which means it is the most generic one. Ask for the strange version on purpose, then cultivate one piece of it. A cut to the stool of a coppiced tree is a provocation, not an instruction, and the tree answers with something no one specified. Use the model the same way.
5
Every image carries its prompt
Keep the prompt history for every image you use. What you asked, what came back, what you changed and why. An image without its history cannot go in the ledger, and an image that is not in the ledger cannot go on the map.
❯ step 1 · write the scorethree to five rules

Start from your one question and from what Assignment 2 taught you about this reach, especially the disagreement you found between your own layer and an instrument. That gap is often the best place to put a rule, because it is where your reach is doing something no single source describes.

Write one line of starting conditions, what you change on day one. Then write three to five rules. Each rule has three parts.

1
A trigger
A condition one of your instruments can read. When knot1 stage passes 820 ft. When the first frost date in the Open-Meteo forecast arrives. When iNaturalist records a heron within the quad. If no instrument on your panel can see the trigger, name the one you would add and mark the rule speculative.
2
An operation
What happens. Cut, plant, open, release, close a path, let a bank erode, stop mowing, do nothing on purpose. Operations, not outcomes. The rule says what you do. The river decides what it becomes.
3
A place
Where on your quad the operation happens, drawn on the map as a point, line or area. Every rule has a geometry, and every geometry stays inside your bounds.
A score is not a plan in disguise. If your rules only make sense when they produce one particular drawing, you have written a plan and called it a score. Test each rule by asking what it would do in a year you did not expect. A good rule still means something in that year.
Feed the modelPaste your one question, your meta.json and the disagreement paragraph from your source assessment. Ask for ten candidate rules, each with a trigger your panel can read, and ask for three of them to be ones you would not have thought of. Use any of the ten, develop your own, or both. Keep only the ones you can defend, and rewrite every one you keep in your own words.
❯ step 2 · perform itthree conditions, three futures

A score is only known by its performances. Have the model perform yours three times, under three conditions, and make one future from each.

The model does not know your river. It has no hydraulics, no sediment budget and no record of what lives in your quad. Left alone it will tell a fluent story about a river like yours, and that story is the same generic picture the image models make, only in words. What it can do is reason from the files you give it. So treat the performance as a test of your rules. Are they clear enough to be performed, and what do they set in motion that you did not see coming? You decide whether the river would actually do that here.

conditionwhat it asks
an ordinary yearThe river as your gauge record reports it, regulated, seasonal, unremarkable. Take the stage range from the gauge data in your data/ folder. What do your rules do when nothing dramatic happens?
a flood yearknot1 at major stage, 830 ft. Minor is 820 and action is 817. If your data holds a past rise, use how fast it came up. Which rules fire, in what order, and what does the bank look like when the water goes down?
fifty years untendedYour starting conditions, then no one comes back. Start from the species iNaturalist records in your quad and the land cover under it. What does the reach build on its own from what you set in motion?

Each future is two things together. An image of the reach in that condition, generated or drawn. And the operation that produced it, a section, a plan diagram or a sequence showing which rules fired and what they did. Then read the narration the way you read a source in Assignment 2. Which sentences can your data hold up, and which are speculation you are choosing to make? Mark each future both ways.

No image without its operation. Image models are very good at a particular picture, lush and sunlit and vaguely sustainable, that could be anywhere. That picture says nothing about this reach. An image earns its place only when it sits beside the drawing of the rules that made it, and when something in it could only be true of your 2 km.
Feed the modelPaste the files, not just the score. Your score and the condition, your meta.json, the files in your data/ folder, your own layer and your disagreement paragraph. Ask it to narrate the performance before it draws anything. Which rule fires first, what the water does, what moves, what grows. Ask it to name the file behind every event in the narration, and to mark as asserted anything it cannot trace to one. Then ask what it would need to know to perform your rules properly that is not in these files. That list is the speculation in your future, and it is often where step 3 begins, with what would tell you. Then ask for the image from that narration, not from your title. Ask for the version that surprises you, and keep the prompt history.
❯ step 3 · choosea hypothesis in spatial form

Now judge your own performances. Which future would you cultivate, and which would you design against? Answer three questions in a few sentences each.

1
Resist, recover or transform
Is your score built to hold the reach as it is, to bring it back quickly after disturbance, or to let it become something new? Say which, and say what that costs.
2
What would tell you
Which instrument, on your panel or one you would add, would show which of your three futures is actually unfolding, and how long it would take to see it. This is what makes the proposition a hypothesis rather than a wish.
3
Fit for whom
Who and what does this reach serve under your score, and who or what bears the cost? A heron, a mussel bed, a person fishing from the bank, the dam operator, the neighbor downstream.
❯ step 4 · the corridorgives downstream, asks upstream

Your reach does not end at its bounds. Water, sediment, seeds, fish and people all cross the line. Site numbers run with the river, so the reach above yours is the number before yours and the reach below is the number after. Site 1 asks the Holston and the French Broad. Site 18 gives to the river beyond the corridor.

Write two lines. Each one has three parts, what crosses the line, when it crosses, and the sentence itself in your words.

For what, choose one of water, sediment, species, people, debris or temperature. Species covers seeds and fish as well as birds, and people covers a path or a trail. For when, choose one of ordinary year, flood year, fifty years untended or always. The first three are the conditions from step 2, and always is for something that crosses every year. The short list is how the corridor map finds your neighbor. The sentence is how we know what you meant.

↓
Gives downstream
What your score sends to the next reach. Sediment released in a flood year. Cooler water from a shaded bank. A seed source. A trail that continues. Be specific about what and when.
↑
Asks upstream
What your score needs from the reach above. Clear water, or sediment. A corridor for a species to arrive along. A bank that does not send debris at the wrong moment.

After Thursday's outcomes page goes up, open your upstream neighbor's map and read what is there. You do not have to agree with them. On the corridor map, every place where one reach gives what the next one asks for will show as a link, and every place where they collide will show as a conflict. A conflict is not a mistake. It is the corridor telling us something no single reach could.

❯ step 5 · map it and export itproposition.geojson

Add the proposition to the map you built in Assignment 2, at the same URL. One new layer toggle, proposition, shows your rules as geometry on the quad. A viewer shows your three futures, each image beside its operation drawing. Keep your Assignment 2 layers. The proposition sits on top of what you learned, it does not replace it.

Then export the proposition as one file in a shared shape. This is how eighteen scores become one corridor.

Do not type this file by hand. Every coordinate comes from the geometry you drew on your map, exported as GeoJSON, or from your meta.json bounds. Paste the example above, your meta.json, the GeoJSON of your drawn rules and your written score, and ask for proposition.geojson.

Then check three things before you trust it. Open the file in geojson.io and see that your rules land on your quad and nowhere else. Open each image URL in a private window and see that it loads. Read every text field and see that the words are yours.

Images live on your site. Put each image in your repo or folder, no wider than 1600 pixels, and use its full https URL in the file. An image that only exists in a chat window will not be on the corridor map.

❯ cat hard.mdthe trap

The temptation is to fall in love with one beautiful future and write the rules backwards from it. That is a plan, and a plan is the thing this river has spent a century refusing.

The harder and better work is to write rules you believe in and then be genuinely curious about what they do, including the futures you did not want. A score that only ever produces the picture you hoped for has not been performed. It has been rendered.

Be daring in the rules and honest in the ledger. The creativity is in what you set in motion. The rigor is in saying, every time, which parts of the future the data can hold up and which parts are yours.
