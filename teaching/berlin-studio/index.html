three iterations of a territory in transformation
ALAR 801052.52°N · 13.40°EBerlinCantrell

The Berlin Research Studio engaged the territory of Greater Berlin as a living laboratory, examining the city through three successive research frames. Each iteration approached a distinct facet of the city's transformation — its street life, its instruments of surveillance, and its hydrology — through a shared method of territorial research, mapping, and strategic speculation.

The three iterations
Fall 2021
Street Life
The rhythms and occupations of the city's public surface — commerce, mobility, and everyday life.
Fall 2020
Surveillance State
Sensing, security, and the technological apparatus that reads and manages the city.
Fall 2022
Berlin Wet City
Cultivated wildness and the city's water — hydrology as an instrument of landscape.

About the studio

The Berlin Research Studio (ALAR 8010) was a graduate research studio taught by Bradley Cantrell, using the city of Berlin as a test bed for territorial-scale landscape research. Across three years, the studio treated Berlin's evolving landscape — its street life, its surveillance infrastructure, and its hydrology — as a subject for mapping, modeling, and strategic design speculation. Each iteration is documented below with its own syllabus and project briefs.

Faculty: Bradley Cantrell, Commonwealth Professor of Landscape Architecture.
