The second phase will be an iterative exploration of new coastal infrastructures and the connection between sensing, monitoring, and feedback. This work should engage hybrid approaches that oscillate between analog and digital techniques of modeling or simulation. The prototypes should be connected to an evolving onto-cartography and may engage coastal conditions from the micro to macro (molecular to global). This form of research engages making and prototyping that feeds back into the Phase 01 deliverables. The coastal prototypes should be refined during this phase, paying close attention to the details of interaction between site processes (visualized through the onto-cartography) and infrastructural choreography (evolved through models).

Sensing, Monitoring & Response

The prototypes will focus on the relationship between sensing, monitoring, and response. An essential question must be explored as the coastal prototypes evolve, a question surrounding the role of humanity in the manipulation of ecological systems. The interaction between responsive infrastructure and the ecological systems that are slowly integrated will question our perception of ecological systems as we understand the aesthetic, performative, and systemic qualities of biology as a curated medium. The view of infrastructure as a modulator, disrupting surrounding systems forms a relationship of opposition and/or scarcity. If you are examining a robust ecology how does infrastructure interrupt larger systems? What can it contribute? How does the infrastructural prototype function as a node within a large network?

Baseline & Datum

The design of the prototypes can take on many forms, both physical and digital but should fundamentally address a need to test and access dynamic processes in relation to site and time. Prototyping and experimentation require a baseline or datum to compare and contrast successive iterations. This baseline could be conceived of as a canvas or standard condition that is used to measure or interact with dynamic processes. The interplay between the baseline and dynamic condition serves as the foundation for developing a proposal in 4 dimensions.

Method

The prototyping phase will explore the deconstruction of site processes and their manipulation through physical, digital, or hybrid prototypes. Each prototype will be iterative and will build a knowledgebase for an evolving design process. The second phase will further develop and refine one prototype to create a performative prototype that utilizes one or more data sets or data sources. The parameters of the prototype and data structure should additionally be constructed to further subsequent strategies and proposals. The prototype will become both a design tool and method for illustrating and observing dynamic processes. The prototype will need to be accessible, functioning, and grounded.

The modeling process should be based on a known method(s) that can be traced through time. This may be a historic process or a nascent modeling technique that could be deconstructed and adapted to an iterative design approach. Photography, stop motion animation, digital rendering, and film may be used to observe, measure, analyze and form conclusions in relation to change over time.

Deliverables

The Phase 02 prototypes and documentation will be presented on May 11th. Presentations will include a refinement of the midterm research and onto-cartography, working prototypes/proof of concept models, and documentation of the devices.
