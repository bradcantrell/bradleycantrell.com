Module III · Adaptive Strategies

adaptation via prototyping

Download the full Module III brief (PDF)

…once married with architecture, mobility, and landscape, infrastructure can more meaningfully integrate territories, reduce marginalization and segregation, and stimulate new forms of interaction. It can then truly become 'landscape.'

Kelly Shannon and Marcel Smets — The Landscape of Contemporary Infrastructure

The Central Valley is a diverse and complex landscape, functioning at every scale, crystallized across a static framework of infrastructure. Canals connecting disparate watersheds, pumps pushing water uphill to defy gravity, pollinators transported for crops, thousands of kilometers of levees, irrigation technology that repatterns a region — a cyborg of contemporary humanity. The infrastructural framework is bold, produced via applied capital to transform the hydrologic, climatic, and ecological regimes of an entire region. Its success relies on predictability, models that define the future via abstraction and simplification. The friction unraveling the Central Valley is situated where the environmental dynamics of this region confront the stasis of an aging technological, political, and social infrastructure.

Stasis is the defining factor of this framework, set within a viscous and vigorous environment that demands change and erases history. To operate within this region requires the embrace of indeterminacy, calculated failure, and methods to harness feedback. A design strategy that relies on adaptation via prototyping, framing the landscape as a laboratory to test the effects of systems at scale. To have agency in this region each project must propose a strategy that addresses:

Emergence

  • How does the project become a rich, complex landscape?
  • What are the key strategic moves that are necessary, and how might this lead to multiple possible futures?
  • How do each of the interventions or interactions aggregate into the definition and curation of territory?

Adaptation

  • In what ways is adaptation designed into the strategy?
  • In what ways does the project take on the complexity of landscape contingencies to evolve over time?
  • How does failure and success play a role in adaptation?
  • How do goals, in terms of design intent and performance, play a role in adaptation?
  • In an indeterminate environment what is being adapted?

Maintenance

  • How is maintenance defined at this scale?
  • What are the goals of maintenance in the project? How does it relate to an evolving, adaptive landscape?
  • What are the methods of evaluation for the maintenance of the territory?
  • How does phasing of the project play a role in its evolution and development?

These are not terms or questions to merely answer, but each should be inherent in the strategies that are developed.

Position

Each team has formed a position via the manifesto from Module I and it must be refined and clearly stated as a driver for the work in Module III. Approach this by giving the project a title, developing a statement (1 sentence or less), and writing a 200–300 word manifesto that clearly articulates the position the team is taking. Revisit your original video (30–60 seconds) to convey a clear narrative for the position.

Topic

The topic for each group was defined in the Module I Atlas and should be the basis of the research moving forward. The topic can and should evolve as new information comes to light. Revisit the Atlas and revise as needed to develop a clear and understandable topic for the final phase of the semester.

Implementation + Speculation

The projects are speculative and function at scales that are difficult to comprehend. To ground the projects it is necessary to articulate how the projects are implemented — through detail drawings showing how materials are assembled, or a long term management plan linked to a city's political process. Use implementation as a way to push speculation through refinement and iteration.

Method

The method was explored in Module II and is critical to the development of the project in Module III. One aspect of the method is required: the creation of a live model that animates the critical issues/phenomena at play in the Central Valley, projected on the milled model — demonstrating an evolving, oscillating, or fluctuating environment with multiple possible futures, annotated with formational logics, ecological dynamics, staging strategies, and adaptation over time.

Deliverables

Required

  • Refined Atlas from Module I, with additional drawings and diagrams from Modules II and III added
  • Refined Video Manifesto (40–60 sec)
  • Project Title, Statement (1 sentence or less) and a Manifesto (~200–300 words)
  • Live Model Animation/Projection

Sample deliverables

  • Hydrological, Ecological, Programmatic timeline
  • Site research and contextual plan diagramming
  • Generative Framework Map and/or Plan
  • Global prototype
  • Catalogs of landscape, programmatic, and building components
  • Physical / Sectional / Overall System Models
  • Overall System Axon, Map, and Sections
  • Serial Sections
  • Oscillation of the overall system (animation and screenshots)
  • Temporal Exploration and Illustrative Vignettes
  • Motion — looping videos, animations of solar exposure, climate, and site use through time
