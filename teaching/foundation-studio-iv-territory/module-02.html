"No computer on Earth can match the processing power of even the most simple natural system, be it of water molecules on a warm rock, a rudimentary enzyme system, or the movement of leaves in the wind. The most powerful and challenging use of the computer…is learning how to make a simple organization (the computer) model what is intrinsic about a more complex, infinitely entailed organization (the natural or real system)."

Sanford Kwinter — "The Computational Fallacy"

In Module II, you will develop an experimental and iterative methodology for testing prototypical interventions and projected scenarios. The chosen mechanism for this study will be a series of live models of the Central Valley — site prototypes which speculate on emergent effects on the territory and various strategies for implementation.

Development of the live models should consider not just the techniques deployed in the creation of the model, but also how these techniques and their presentational aspects consider the linkages between the physical world and the surrogate (model). The intent of the live models is not so much to create a scientifically realistic simulation but rather to provoke speculation and ideation, as well as creating a compelling format for communicating novel ideas and complex strategies. Your chosen live modeling approach should be understood within a broader context that is aware of the history and past applications of the technique (precedent models), its pros and cons, and novel approaches. Namely:

  • What questions are being asked, and how are they tested via the modeling strategy?
  • How do certain techniques focus the outcomes, and how do others expand outcomes?
  • What range of modifications do the prototypes test? Scale, scope, complexity?

Live Modeling Techniques

The live models produced in this module will begin with a CNC-milled foam base model at 1:100,000. Each group should consider the scale(s) of inquiry which makes the most sense for their project, and plan to utilize either a section or the entire foam model of the Central Valley. The following list of live modeling techniques is meant to be suggestive rather than restrictive:

  • Digital projection — projecting maps and animations onto the physical model
  • Augmented reality — combining projection and sensing to interpret changes in the model such as topographic shifts or prototypical interventions
  • Sensing — attaching sensors to the model and testing in different environments/simulations
  • Stop-motion photography — using the model as the basis for analog stop-motion animation
  • Casting — using the foam model as a mold for plaster or concrete casts
  • Chemical processes — burning, acid etching, compound reactions
  • Physical processes — etching, carving, weathering, decay
  • Hydrological simulation — working with water or other fluids to test various landforming operations

Deliverables

  1. Live presentation model utilizing one or more live modeling techniques with milled foam model.

  2. Atlas Part II, documenting:

    • Technical documentation of the modeling process — an "instructional guide" using diagrams to document techniques, including process photos
    • Intervention prototypes (5–10), showing technical drawings/diagrams and site drawings (plans/sections) illustrating how the prototype engages the landscape/site
    • Speculative drawings of landscape alterations
    • Potential sites of operation (3 or more), or "proving grounds" for testing your prototypical intervention — priority sites to visit on the studio trip
