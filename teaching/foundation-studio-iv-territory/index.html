Territory · Landscape

An abstracted space composed of objects and processes for state administration. Borders, census tracts, tax parcels. Landscape is the living medium, an always-evolving array of materials, spaces, relationships, and histories, composed and choreographed to produce life-catalyzing forms. The studio positions California's Central Valley as both subject and instrument — a testing ground for adaptive infrastructure, where the valley itself becomes a prototype. It aims to conceptualize and articulate the territory as adaptive infrastructure, a landscape in flux as it responds to changing environmental, programmatic, market, and sociocultural conditions.

“Wildness is undomesticated rather than untouched — design priorities change from maintaining a precious and pure environment to creating plural conditions of autonomy and distributed control.”

The three modules
Module I · 25%
Unpacking the Valley
Research, mapping, and modeling — establishing the iterative and experimental methodology of the studio.
Module II · 25%
Live Models, Surrogates and Prototypes
Live models of the Central Valley — site prototypes speculating on emergent territorial effects.
Module III · 50%
Adaptive Strategies
A comprehensive design strategy for the Central Valley as a dynamic and evolving landscape.

About the studio

Foundation Studio IV is a fourth-semester foundation studio taught across the Department of Landscape Architecture. The Prototyping the Territory iteration engaged California's Central Valley — treating the valley as a landscape experiment, a place for ongoing research and knowledge production where design propositions ask questions as much as they provide answers.

Faculty: Bradley Cantrell (Professor and Chair), Andrea Hansen-Phillips (Assistant Professor), and Brad Goetz (Assistant Professor). Teaching Fellow: Zihao Zhang. Meeting: Tuesday and Thursday, 12:30–18:00.

This page documents the Central Valley iteration of the LAR 7020 studio series (Spring 2019 and 2020). The Chesapeake Bay iteration, Prototyping the Bay, is documented separately.
