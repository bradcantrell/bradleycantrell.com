This studio aims to conceptualize and articulate the territory as an adaptive infrastructure, the landscape in a state of flux as it responds to changing environmental, programmatic, market, and sociocultural conditions. The studio places emphasis on the contingent, the provisional, and the conditional, and asks students to amplify productive instabilities while inventing new types of landscape form. Landscape is our starting point for the project of generating and maintaining the territory.

Consequently, this studio will use landscape both as medium and as mechanism for testing and prototyping ideas on the making of the territory. Rather than understanding them as opposites, it will emphasize connections and hybridizations of systems and environmental dynamics, and it will utilize working methodologies that privilege experimentation and iterative modeling — playful and critical prototyping — that could suggest pathways toward new landscape-based approaches to territorial systems.

The Central Valley

The image of the Central Valley is that of a productive and robust agricultural paradise, the product of hard work and ingenuity providing much of North America and the world with the food products we desire. Beneath this façade is a withering corpse, slowly drying, sinking, and eroding due to exploitation, settlement, and blunt engineering. Within this territory exists a latent wildness; remote areas that are difficult to access, novel ecological systems, hyper productive agriculture, sites of extraction, and powerful hydrologic infrastructures.

The condition of the Central Valley is dire and our work as designers is not to find a solution that "restores" or "saves" this marginalized environment. The studio will engage the valley as a test bed that is examined, poked, and prodded through remote and mediated methods of observation and modification. The remote view of the valley will be assumed as the norm; the site as experienced through human senses will be only one factor put alongside an array of other modes of mediated experience, data collection, and monitoring.

Thinking of the valley itself as the model and the landscape as the media, the studio will take a critical look at the long history of modeling and simulation that has driven the practice of civil engineering, developing protocols to choreograph processes and modes of feedback rather than assume the methods of modeling and representation need ever greater precision.

Lastly the research will engage the technologies that have created the valley in its current form — the hydrological infrastructure, the monitoring systems, the agricultural machinery, and the logistics infrastructures — to imagine how new technologies (machine learning, artificial intelligence, simulation, and robotics) can play a future role in the reformulation of a managed Central Valley.

Module I: Unpacking the Valley

Module I seeks to unpack the issues framed above through simultaneous research, mapping, and modeling. Its objective is twofold: 1) to become familiar with the Central Valley through a variety of lenses, and 2) to establish the iterative and experimental methodology of the studio.

Module II: Live Models, Surrogates and Prototypes

In Module II, you will develop an experimental and iterative methodology, the result of which will be a series of live models of the Central Valley — site prototypes which speculate on emergent effects on the territory and various strategies for implementation. These models will build upon the physical topographical watershed models produced in Module I through mechanisms of your choosing designed to demonstrate strategic change, cause-and-effect, and indeterminate outcomes.

Module III: Adaptive Strategies

Module III asks teams to develop a comprehensive strategy for the Central Valley that is the product of the manifesto and an evolving design methodology. Teams will consider the Central Valley as a dynamic and evolving landscape, shaped by multiple agents — human/non-human, geologic, biologic, hydrologic. Each team will evolve and refine the modeling methods developed in Module II to generate a strategic approach and future vision of the Central Valley.

Course Objectives

  1. As a team, develop a coherent design manifesto that speaks to your convictions regarding the social and philosophical basis of your design research.
  2. Identify methods of intervention that engage territorial systems through the medium and tools of landscape architecture.
  3. Produce models and representational strategies that allow for a deeper engagement with landscape processes and modes of feedback.
  4. Formulate landscape strategies that capitalize on landscape as infrastructure through interventions that are emergent and adaptive.
