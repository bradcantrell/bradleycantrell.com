Territory · Landscape

An abstracted space composed of objects and processes for state administration. Borders, census tracts, tax parcels. Landscape is the living medium, an always-evolving array of materials, spaces, relationships, and histories, composed and choreographed to produce life-catalyzing forms. The studio positions the Chesapeake Bay as both subject and instrument — a testing ground for adaptive infrastructure, where the bay itself becomes a prototype. It aims to conceptualize and articulate the territory as adaptive infrastructure, a landscape in flux as it responds to changing environmental, programmatic, market, and sociocultural conditions.

“Wildness is undomesticated rather than untouched — design priorities change from maintaining a precious and pure environment to creating plural conditions of autonomy and distributed control.”

About the studio

Foundation Studio IV is a fourth-semester foundation studio taught across the Department of Landscape Architecture. The Spring 2025 iteration, Prototyping the Bay, engaged the coastal landscapes and islands of Pocomoke Sound in the Chesapeake Bay — treating the bay as a landscape experiment, a place for ongoing research and knowledge production where design propositions ask questions as much as they provide answers.

Recipient of the Inaugural Tulane Climate Curriculum Prize.

Faculty: Bradley Cantrell (Professor and Chair) and Leena Cho (Associate Professor). Instructional Assistants: Joyce Fong and Samantha Hubbard. Meeting: Tuesday and Thursday, 12:30–17:00.
