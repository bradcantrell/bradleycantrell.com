Project 2 · Adaptation

a synthetic, cyborg ecology for the Louisiana Gulf Coast

“…Adaptive Architecture will include a focus on computational design of environmentally responsive, intelligent, interactive, and reconfigurable architecture.”

ACADIA 2013 — Adaptive Architecture · Cambridge, Ontario

The interface between the constructed environment and ecological systems is slowly blurring new strategies in urbanism, biological engineering, and technological interfaces. Your final project will explore the design potential of a synthetic, cyborg ecology, one that generates responsive infrastructures and environments. In the most basic sense responsive technologies refers to the interaction between devices, users, and the environment usually through methods of sensing and feedback.

As students of landscape architecture pursuing research in the Louisiana Gulf Coast, your project will focus primarily on responsive technologies that develop a relationship between digital constructs (virtual and physical) and biological systems. This view of ecological systems, through the lens of responsive technologies, posits that the designer is responsible for the creation and implementation of processes that curate, nudge, and sculpt landscape systems.

The project will speak to the call for the 2013 ACADIA Conference in Cambridge, Ontario, Canada. The seminar will approach this project in pairs to develop a proposal for a synthetic ecology that facilitates human settlement within the Louisiana Gulf Coast. The project will consist of 3 phases — a topical presentation, a 1500–2000 word work-in-progress paper, and a project proposal.

Phase 1 · Topical Presentation Due March 18

Each group will prepare a 15 minute presentation (PDF or PPT) on one of the topics listed below. The presentation will cover a brief history and speculate on design applications. The presentation may use a compilation of original and others’ visual work and must be fully cited.

  • Domestication / Selective Breeding
  • Climate Modification
  • Terraforming
  • Genetic Engineering
  • Commercial Hydroponics / Synthetic Growing Systems

Phase 2 · Work-in-Progress Research Paper Due April 17

Each group will begin their proposal through typical design iteration. This proposal will be situated within the broader work of physical computing, interactive design, ecological systems, and synthetic biology. The proposal will be illustrated and described as a work in progress in a 1500–2000 word illustrated paper. The paper should thoroughly describe the project, illustrate key features/relationships, and situate the work in the discipline. The ACADIA 2013 call for submissions will provide guidance for the submission.

  • Pin-up — March 18
  • Paper First Draft — due March 25
  • Second Draft — due April 8
  • Paper Submitted — April 17

Phase 3 · Refined Project Proposal Due May 6

The proposal will be refined through design prototyping (physical and/or virtual), experimentation, and/or simulation methods. The refined proposal will take on a visual presentation with prints (2 portrait 24″ × 36″ required), models (physical/virtual), and/or animations. The refined proposal will be submitted as a research poster for ACADIA 2013.

  • Refined Illustrations, Pin-up — due April 15
  • Pin-up — April 22
  • Pin-up — Optional, April 29
