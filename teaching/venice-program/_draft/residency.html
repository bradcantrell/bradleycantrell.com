Italy Residency

The Residency

You will live in the lagoon for seven weeks. This is not a field trip — a brief visit that extracts observations and returns to the studio. It is a residency: an extended inhabitation that allows design thinking to develop in continuous dialogue with the territory it addresses. The lagoon is not a subject visited but a condition inhabited.

All four courses are taught within the residency. There are no fixed course times. Instead, each week is organized as a unified program: field exercises, studio desk crits, lectures, site visits, and independent work distributed across Monday through Friday. Weekends are yours. Venice is thirty minutes by vaporetto. The Biennale runs through November. The Dolomites are two hours north.

Mornings in the vineyard fog. Midday light on the barene. Evening tide against the fondamente. These are not background. They are primary material.

Accommodations

Vicenza is the program's academic base. The Valmarana Center hosts reviews, lectures, and provides workspace and meeting facilities. It is the institutional home of the program in Italy. The Module 02 Review is held here.

Venice is where students live during the residency. Apartments are located in the city, providing direct access to the lagoon, the Biennale, and the urban fabric that is the subject of study. There is no single program building in Venice. The city itself is the classroom.

Combo Venezia (Cannaregio) serves as the Venice gathering point for studio desk crits, invited lectures, and program meetings. This hostel and cultural space provides flexible work areas where the full group convenes during the residency.

Daily Rhythm

Mornings are for independent work: drawing, writing, revisiting sites, or the formal sessions scheduled for that day. The lagoon rewards early attention — light, tide, and bird activity peak in the morning hours.

Midday typically holds structured program activities: field exercises, studio desk crits, faculty-led site visits, or lectures. These are the anchor points around which the day organizes itself.

Afternoons continue program activities or open into independent work time. Studio space is always available. Faculty hold regular office hours for individual and team consultations.

Evenings allow for processing: compiling field documentation, advancing design work, and the informal conversations that often produce the most productive insights. Group dinners on Mazzorbo are a regular occurrence.

Weekly Rhythm

Each week of the residency follows a loose pattern: field exercises anchor Mondays or Fridays, providing direct engagement with the territory that informs the week's work. Midweek holds studio sessions, lectures, and desk crits. The specific distribution varies by week and is detailed on the unified schedule.

Field exercises are weekly structured engagements with the lagoon, designed to develop practices of observation, recording, and interpretation. They are led by different faculty each week and draw on all four courses. Content will be developed over the summer and added to the schedule as it solidifies.

Weekends are free. The residency is intensive. Sustained attention requires rest. The lagoon is available whenever you choose to engage it, and some of the most valuable work happens on a Saturday morning walk when no one is watching.

Equipment

Drawing Kit
Sketchbook (A5, waterproof paper), graphite pencils, waterproof pens, watercolor travel set, folding ruler. Drawing is the primary field instrument for seven weeks.
Documentation
Camera or phone with adequate storage, extra batteries, power bank, audio recorder for ambient sound and interviews.
Navigation
Printed maps, compass, GPS device, waterproof map case. You will learn the lagoon without GPS, but it remains essential for precise documentation.
Field Clothing
Waders or waterproof boots (essential), rain jacket, layers for variable autumn weather (10–20°C), sun protection.
Studio Materials
Sketch models, chipboard, cutting tools, adhesives. Physical modeling continues throughout the residency.
Personal
Passport, visa if required, health insurance, medications, comfortable walking shoes, a notebook that is not your sketchbook.
You cannot understand the lagoon from a desk. You have to feel the tide pull against your boots, taste the salt on the wind, watch a Dorona vine find its way through soil that should not support life, and do these things not once but across weeks that become a way of living.
