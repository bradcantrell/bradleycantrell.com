Studio Brief — Phase 03

The third phase of the term will be a focusing of the project and expansion of the manifesto. This will require that each project assess how it performs in relation to topic vs. method and position vs. implementation. The manifesto plays a role in Phase 03 as it is a way to synthesize a current view on the discipline of landscape architecture. The manifesto will situate your intellectual curiosities and the project investigates a select portion of this domain.

Topic vs. Method

Topic and method are always present and interrelated in any design process; this studio has asked you to follow methods before fully developing the topic(s). The first two phases were methodological, the last portion of the studio places emphasis on the topic of each project (enabling the work to achieve a certain level of radicalization). With this combination of approaches, you should aspire to produce a speculative and radical project that is competently grounded.

When working with a method, you are tasked with following it correctly. On the other hand, when working with a topic, you must get it right. In this last phase of the studio, you are responsible for demonstrating that you are developing your topic by continuing to push the project in the appropriate direction:

  • What is the thesis of your proposal and how does it interrogate your manifesto? Radicalize your argument in order to frame your project as precisely as possible.
  • Since this studio tackles the question of wildness, the technological, and the cultural, how is this addressed in your project and how is the term neo-organism defined in your project? What does wetware mean and how does it relate to the neo-organism?
  • How is landscape used as a medium for imagining and enabling a new type of infrastructure? Continue to develop your project while instrumentalizing landscape as the medium to imagine your definition of the neo-organism and associated wetware.

Position vs. Implementation

The intellectual position from where you are approaching the disciplinary question of infrastructure and wildness needs to be clearly defined and argued for in your final presentation. Consequently, the design decisions that you have taken in order to implement your project need to also be clearly defined:

  • What landscapes, infrastructures, and technologies can be considered precedents for your project? What are the projects that should be part of the conversation when discussing your project?
  • What are the design decisions that you have taken in order to shape and implement your vision? Continue to develop your project by placing emphasis in those decisions that are of crucial importance to your design.
  • What constitutes rigor in the context of your project? What are the main design factors that have to be precisely executed in order to implement your project? Diagram them. Label them clearly in your drawings.
  • What kind of wildness, infrastructure, or methodology are you arguing for? Consider making historical and/or theoretical arguments that frame your project within precise social, political, cultural, and/or economic frameworks.

Development vs. Presentation

In the first and second phase of the semester, you focused on the development of the project satisfying methodological requirements for prototyping and mapping. In this third phase of the semester, how your project is presented will be your responsibility:

  • What is the image of this landscape, infrastructure, or method? Produce a compelling set of views, animations, or models.
  • What kind of representation techniques are the most appropriate for your project? Continue to explore how to best represent wildness, data, flux, and humanity as suggested by your project.
  • What is the aesthetic you are developing and how does it advance your narrative?
  • What is the relationship between the onto-cartographic representation, the micro-cosm, and the physical site?
  • What documents, models, and animations are necessary to present your project?

Objectives

  1. To develop a landscape-based strategy for framing the concept of the neo-organism and wetware.
  2. To develop more specific and detailed relationships between wildness, technology, and culture in the landscape.
  3. To argue for the proposed neo-organism through techniques of iteration and critical comparison.
  4. To argue for a particular kind of infrastructure through the ways by which the proposed neo-organism is drawn and modeled.

Deliverables

The manifesto is due on April 30th and should be 800–1200 words in length with 1–2 accompanying images. The Phase 03 deliverables will be due on May 7th at 22:00 and then presented at the final review on May 8th.
