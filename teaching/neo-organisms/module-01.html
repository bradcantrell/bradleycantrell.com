Studio Brief — Phase 01

The first phase involves site research and the construction of an onto-cartography that maps site phenomena, processes, and interactions. This onto-cartography will function as the relational diagram that will guide the micro-cosm (testing grounds) you develop next. During this first phase students will also experiment with prototypes to develop their micro-cosm methodology. The work should develop a rigorous definition of site and how the implementation of mediated technologies and adaptive systems might interface with processes.

Onto-Cartography

The term onto-cartography comes from Levi Bryant’s assertion that everything can be construed as a machine with specific interactions with other machines. Machines in this sense are multi-layered, simple/complex, and open/closed. Most importantly, machines represent not only physical elements but also bodies of knowledge, ideologies, politics, and/or economies. The onto-cartographic framework is rooted in object oriented ontology and presumes that the object (machines) and its relationships with other objects (machines) constitute the whole.

Micro-Cosm

In conjunction with the onto-cartographic site research a micro-cosm will be developed to test, prototype, and generate protocols (wetware) for future neo-organisms (infrastructures). Conceptually, the micro-cosm is a self-contained environment with limited connections to the external environment. The introduction of new elements, or modifications of the system, can then be monitored and observed to understand its effects. To use this as a generative/prototyping device it will be necessary to expand this definition while maintaining connections to the micro-cosm as a concept. During this first phase, students should experiment with digital and analog models, animations, and/or drawing methods that test the micro-cosm concept. The micro-cosm will create a methodology that engages media as a performative act — a methodology to develop protocols for interaction and to find moments of chance and reveal the unexpected through rigorous and well documented experiments.

Site

The onto-cartography will focus on sites within Southern Louisiana that have been identified in the 2012–2017 Louisiana Coastal Master Plan. There is latitude in the selection of a site and may be expanded from the master plan if necessary. Based on the range of interventions proposed in the 2012–2017 Master Plan, the site research, onto-cartography, and micro-cosm should outline a framework and methodology to propose a new coastal infrastructure. The term infrastructure is used broadly as a way to describe the design of landscape that is integral to the environment in which it resides. It is important that this work forms a clear trajectory for the second phase of semester.

The choice of media and technique is an open question for each student when developing the onto-cartography and prototyping the micro-cosm. The onto-cartography should intelligently represent existing and proposed relationships between objects (machines) within the site:

  • What is the relationship between the onto-cartographic representation, the micro-cosm, and the physical site?
  • How can this representation promote iterative prototyping for design exploration?
  • How does the onto-cartography perform as a virtualized process, and how can it guide the micro-cosm?
  • In what way does the micro-cosm methodology provide tangible access to a site process or processes and allow the designer to observe, manipulate, or interact with this process?

Deliverables

The Phase 01 deliverables will be presented on February 1st. Typical deliverables for the research and onto-cartography will be models, video/animation, mappings, and/or diagrams. The initial micro-cosm prototypes may be diagrams, drawings, digital or physical models, and/or simulations used to explain how the micro-cosm would function as a generative and/or analytical tool for design.
