Premise

This advanced research seminar will explore animating and modeling ephemeral ecological systems. Students will be asked to research ecological processes or constructed systems and deconstruct the process through modeling and animation. Students will learn techniques necessary to model ephemeral processes such as wind, fire, earth and water using particle systems and other techniques that are at times simulations and at other times illustrations. Digital representation will be combined with field observations of ecological processes through recording methods such as video, photography, sensing and sketching in order to fully understand the complex processes that will be illustrated.

As a research seminar students will become experts on specific software, representation techniques, and their chosen ecological system. This information will be documented at lab.visual-logic.com to serve as a resource for the seminar and outside collaborators.

Pedagogical Objectives

  1. Develop an observation method to document ephemeral ecological systems.
  2. Clearly define the scope of an ecology or ecological system.
  3. Create a comprehensive body of research, including scientific and anecdotal evidence to describe an ecological system.
  4. Design an exhibit using analog and digital media to express the intricacies of the ephemeral ecologies.
  5. Document the representation and research process.
  6. Develop a representation method and document the procedures.
  7. Apply knowledge from research to the representation and simulation process.

Procedure

Students will work in pairs to complete all phases of work during the semester: ecological system research, software/tool review, technique documentation and the exhibition. All sources must be cited.

  • Ecological System Research — define an area of research focused on a specific ecological system and create a body of research to support the representation of its components.
  • Software/Tool Review — choose a specific digital tool and research and review its ability to create ecological illustrations.
  • Technique Documentation — document two representation techniques, showing the representation process step-by-step.
  • Exhibition — represent the researched ecological system as a framework, process, and experience.

All of the work in the seminar was documented at lab.visual-logic.com. Each student had an account on the website and was required to contribute to their group research, review, and documentation.
