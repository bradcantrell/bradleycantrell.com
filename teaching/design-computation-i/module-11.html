LAR 6710 DCI Final Brief

Objectives

  1. to develop a clear aesthetic inquiry and representational strategy based upon established precedents
  2. to establish a level of fidelity between analog and digital models
  3. to develop a personal workflow with digital and analog tools for modeling and visualization
  4. to create a comprehensive form of documentation that will serve as a guide for exhibition

Populating the Matrix — Ecotones & Anthromes

Each group is organized by its quadrants, defined by its topography, and situated within an imaginary island. A matrix is “something (such as a situation or a set of conditions) from which something else originates, develops, or takes form”; the island matrix provides a set of conditions from which to originate a new fictional terrain. An ecotone is “a transition area between two adjacent ecological communities”; an anthropogenic biome (“anthrome”) is “a new way to classify terrestrial biomes that integrate (instead of ignore) human interaction with ecosystems, yielding a global map of human altered biomes.”

Keynote landscapes representing anthromes were assigned to the remaining open quadrants within the island matrix, transforming what was once a denuded terrain into an amalgam of biomes, geologies, cultures, and climates. Each keynote landscape can be viewed as a discrete system — the outcome of the interaction between eight metrics: geologic, landform, soil, vegetative, climatic, wildlife, water, and human factors. In coordination with adjacent quadrants, each student surveys their quadrant’s uncharted territory in an attempt to originate new hybrids and represent new ecotones — transitional landscapes where two or more communities meet and integrate.

Quadrant Composite — Installation & Exhibition

The final installation and exhibition requires that each quadrant group devise a nascent visual strategy through a series of composite drawings and models. Each student creates a view of their quadrant, developed alongside a model and diagram. Groups collaborate to establish an aesthetic inquiry and exhibition framework, coordinating style, content, and the sequencing and alignment between quadrant drawings and models, and determining a degree of integration between forms of representation that coincide with the transformation in landscape between quadrants.

Aesthetic Inquiry

As a group, choose an aesthetic associated with the representation of landscape and/or the discipline of landscape architecture, and use the quadrant’s visualization, installation, and exhibition to critically investigate and reinterpret it. An aesthetic is formed by its purpose or objective, the technology or media used for its visualization, and its form of representation (image / model / diagram). During lecture, Brad discussed imperialist versus naturalist aesthetics: an imperialist aesthetic is inclined toward the categorization, analysis, and diagrammatic representation of landscape and ecology (Carl Steinitz), while a naturalist aesthetic is concerned with the expression of landscape as a whole or composite (Alexander von Humboldt’s Naturgemälde).

The group documents the chosen precedents, the overall narrative, an explanation of technique and method, a guide for illustrations, and a material guide for the models — technical documentation used to explain the installation to an external audience.

Exhibition Framework

Each group strategizes how to organize and exhibit each quadrant’s image, diagram, and model within the Naug. Exhibition frameworks can include a sensory experience — illumination, dry ice for fog, wind, diffused scent, recorded sound — and students can coordinate their images by season, scale, or type.

Deliverables

One Image

A photomontage / collage that visualizes select aspects of the quadrant, guided by an aesthetic and developed through a form of obsession with a particular technique. Choose a drawing type — plan, perspective, aerial, section, axon, vignette — and examine particular aspects of the terrain in relation to adjacent quadrants.

One Diagram

A diagrammatic plan that illustrates the surface morphology of the quadrant — land cover (hydrology / vegetation / topography) and behavior (diurnal, cyclical, periodic, habitual, recurring processes of transformation). The drawing is the same dimensions as the midterm physical model (15″ × 15″), coordinated with adjoining sites so systems work across the quadrants within the group, using linework only in a graphical language coherent with the other deliverables.

One Model

A 15″ × 15″ construction of the landscape quadrant, exploring a range of media (wood, plexi, wire, paper, basswood, plaster, wax, resin, high density white foam) and fabrication methods (3D printing, CNC, laser cutter) to represent surface, strata, hydrology, vegetation, and other phenomena. Consider sensory effects that play to the five recognized methods of perception — taste, sight, touch, smell, sound — and the four elements: fire, earth, air, and water.
