In this exercise, you will be asked to dive into sensors to sense a specific phenomenon and to create a real-time visualization of the data using Rhino/Grasshopper or another software such as Processing.

Choose a Phenomenon

You should choose a phenomenon from the following list. In consultation with your instructor you can also specify another phenomenon — for example, you can focus on outdoor CO2 concentration changes, the presence of birds in your backyard, the moisture and composition of sediment on the geomorphology table or soil in Milton Land Lab, etc.

Weather Air Quality Light Water Flows / Level Soil Sound Wind Flame Flora Fauna Human Behavior / Occupancy

Method

Follow these steps to collect and process the sensor data.

Characterize Phenomenon

What are the characteristics of the phenomenon you want to sense? (e.g., how do you define a rainy/sunny day? How is it different from others?) Use images or diagrams to illustrate the characteristics. Do some case studies — what are the related precedents? Include the case studies and sources in your submission.

Decide Sensors

What are the different possible options to measure that phenomenon? e.g., what sensor will be able to detect such a phenomenon? Where would you locate it? Do you need multiple sensors? Can you repurpose other sensors to measure this phenomenon? Use diagrams to illustrate your method. For each of the sensors (limit the number of sensors to three), find application examples and referencing code. Test the code by yourself. You need to include the source, code, and your testing GIFs in your submission.

Process Signal

  • Observe Signals. Show the sensed raw data, observe how the signal changes when the environment changes (e.g., how noisy is it? What’s the range?).
  • Preprocess Signal. Manipulate the signal to be more usable for classification (e.g., filtering noise, normalizing, removing offset).
  • Features Extraction (optional). Find and design the features that will describe the phenomenon the best (e.g., absolute acceleration, magnitude).
  • Build Classifier (optional). Design and build a classifier using the features you designed.

Data Visualization and Logging

Build a customized data visualization for the phenomenon. You can either visualize the real-time sensed data from the field, or recorded samples that you process and visualize later. You can use mapping (2D and 3D), charts, interactive 3D models, interactive visual art, or any customized representations. Include GIFs or videos of the visualization in your submission.
