Introduction

Meeting Time: Monday 9:00–11:30 (hybrid synchronous instruction in 2021; Campbell Hall 425 in 2022).

Faculty: Bradley Cantrell, Chair and Professor of Landscape Architecture, bcantrell@virginia.edu. Xun Liu, Teaching Fellow / Lecturer, PhD Student in Landscape Architecture, xl4xw@virginia.edu.

The interface between technology, biology, and culture is collapsing, requiring a blurring of strategies to address nascent methods of sensing, processing, and prototyping (computational and physical). The course aims to introduce students to techniques of physical computing that can be used to develop design prototypes of tools that visualize and modify landscapes.

Students will create physical prototypes that will engage scaled models (Geomorphology Table) or within the field at Milton Airfield. The first half of the semester will be a series of hands-on workshops to foster the skills necessary in data acquisition, electronics, and coding. The second half will be used to prototype and document projects working individually or in small groups. Previous experience with coding and physical computing is not necessary. The focus will be on the production of knowledge through the process of making and documentation, requiring sustained iterative development and a curiosity to push the interface between landscape and technology.

The Assignment

Students work in pairs (2021) or individually and in small groups (2022) to create a physical computing device — an instrument — that will sense and respond to environmental phenomena. This device will be created to either respond 1:1 with a physical landscape, a site that the student chooses, or be used in a live physical model such as the geomorphology table in 118 Campbell Hall or a physical model that the student or a group of students constructs.

The device will be a physical device that is a proof of concept, meaning that it should perform the basic functions but may also point to or conceptually demonstrate more advanced functions. Each student will also create an interface that will visualize and operate the device. Both the instrument and interface should be developed as design tools, extending the capabilities of the designer.

The assignment also requires documentation of the process. This will require finding precedents and referencing code and the work of others, conceptually representing the need and purpose of the instrument/interface, creation of a tutorial to explain to others how they can also create the instrument and interface, and technical documentation such as drawings, schematics, and in situ drawings of the operation of the instrument and interface. This will be prepared in either a booklet or webpage, a template will be provided.
